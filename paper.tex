\begin{abstract}
The Connes--Consani--Moscovici (CCM) construction produces finite real
symmetric operators whose spectra approximate the ordinates of the
nontrivial Riemann zeta zeros. We develop a caller-facing convergence law for
the first ordinate as a function of the prime cutoff $\lambda^2$, basis size
$N$, and requested precision $P$. Intrinsically, the relative error separates
into finite-basis and finite-cutoff channels,
\[
 E(N,\lambda^2)\le
 \varepsilon_{\mathrm{modes}}(N,\lambda^2)
 +\varepsilon_{\mathrm{primes}}(\lambda^2),
\]
while the observed number of matching decimal digits is, up to a bounded
noncancellation term,
\[
 D(\lambda^2,N,P)=\min\!\left(
 D_{\mathrm{Wprec}}(P),D_{\mathrm{nModes}}(N,\lambda^2),
 D_{\mathrm{Primes}}(\lambda^2)\right)+O(1).
\]
Precision is an instrument-resolution cap, not a third intrinsic error
mechanism. On the calibrated domain $7\le\lambda^2\le100$, the operational
mode submodel is
\[
 D_{\mathrm{nModes}}=D_{\mathrm{Primes}}
 \tanh\!\left[\left(\frac{cN}{\lambda^2\ln\lambda^2}\right)^{q_\gamma(\lambda^2)}\right],
 \qquad
 q_\gamma(\lambda^2)=\frac{2}{2\gamma+\ln\ln\lambda^2},
 \qquad c=\frac4\pi .
\]
With these published constants fixed, an independent numerical
reimplementation reproduces complete ramps at $\lambda^2=7,13,20$ with RMS
errors $0.47$, $0.66$, and $1.18$ digits, respectively. A new four-point
sweep at $\lambda^2=1000$ gives the complementary result: the same closed
$q_\gamma$ submodel overpredicts the first-root accuracy by $118$--$167$
digits. Depending on the observable and fitting convention, the measured
effective exponent lies in the range $0.74$--$0.80$, rather than
$q_\gamma(1000)=0.648$. Thus the three-budget law, the stretched-$\tanh$
ramp family, and the coordinate $\lambda^2\ln\lambda^2$ remain the working
structure, while the large-cutoff dependence of the exponent, onset
coefficient, or transition coordinate is explicitly open.

The finite-cutoff channel has the measured form
$D_{\mathrm{Primes}}=K\lambda^2-P_m\log_{10}\lambda^2-c_m$. Its leading
coefficient $K=4\pi/\ln10$ coincides with the classical fourth-index prolate
singular-value asymptotic; transfer to the constrained CCM $h_0$--$h_4$ trial
state and the reverse inequality remain open. The retained lattice has the
exact edge $T_{\max}=2\pi N/\ln\lambda^2$, and the Riemann--von Mangoldt law
gives a geometric zero-capacity estimate. The empirical law is stated only
for validated matrix assemblies. Explicit rigor tiers separate proved
identities and bounds, conditional root-transfer formulas, calibrated laws,
and open large-cutoff models. This work has been shared with the CCM authors,
including their lead author, Professor Alain Connes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The Connes--Consani--Moscovici (CCM) construction~\cite{CCM2025}, building on
the Connes--van Suijlekom truncated Weil form~\cite{CvS2025}, produces a family
of finite self-adjoint operators $D_{\log}^{(\lambda,N)}$ whose spectra
approximate the imaginary parts $\gamma_k$ of the nontrivial Riemann zeta
zeros to remarkable numerical accuracy: $55$ digits at the headline
configuration of~\cite{CCM2025}, which we extended to $1019.0$ measured matching digits in an
independent reproduction~\cite{AndrewsA}. The construction has two integer-like
control parameters, a prime cutoff $\lambda^2$ (the primes $p\le\lambda^2$
enter the Weil form) and a basis size $N$ (the matrix is $(2N{+}1)\times(2N{+}1)$),
together with the caller-requested precision $P=P_{\mathrm{req}}$ in decimal digits. The rate at
which the eigenvalue converges in the basis size $N$ is named the central open
problem in~\cite{CCM2025}; Groskin~\cite{Groskin2026}
measured a Galerkin convergence exponent empirically but disclaims deriving it,
and \'Sliwi\'nski~\cite{Sliwinski2026} proves an inverse-logarithmic lower bound
on the mean spectral error. We previously
tested two proposed rate laws against high-precision data and found them
unsupported~\cite{AndrewsB}.

The author's starting point is a belief the Hilbert--P\'olya conjecture
already expresses: that apparent randomness in the zeros is structure not yet
written down. The CCM construction gives that belief a computable form. What
remained open is the rate at which the computation converges, and
characterizing that rate explicitly is the subject of this paper.
\begin{definition}\label{def:D}
$D(\lambda^2,N,P) := -\log_{10}\bigl(|\nu_1-\gamma_1|/|\gamma_1|\bigr)$ is the
\emph{matching-digit count}: the number of correct leading decimal digits of the
first Riemann zero $\gamma_1$ reproduced by $\nu_1$, the first ordinate produced
by the CCM construction, computed from the caller-requested decimal target $P=P_{\mathrm{req}}$ via MPFR/GMP. The reference $\gamma_1$ is
computed to $2500$ significant digits by rigorous interval arithmetic
(Arb~\cite{Arb2017}), far exceeding the largest match reported here
($\approx1070$ digits at $\lambda^2=200$), so the reported counts are genuine and
not reference-limited; its leading digits agree with the standard tabulations
of~\cite{Odlyzko}.
\end{definition}

\noindent The same number is read as a \emph{ceiling} or a
\emph{floor} depending on the direction of view. $D_{\mathrm{Primes}}$ is a
ceiling on the achievable digit count and, equally, the depth of the accuracy
floor at the cutoff; both usages refer to the one object.

\noindent Our central result, the \emph{Andrews CCM Convergence Law}, has two
equivalent forms. Its intrinsic (spectral) form is the two-channel decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-intrinsic}
  \boxed{\;E(N,\lambda^2)\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{modes}}(N,\lambda^2)
         +\varepsilon_{\mathrm{primes}}(\lambda^2)\;}
\end{equation}
with the channels defined in Section~\ref{sec:skeleton},
\eqref{eq:channels}. Their signed displacements add exactly before absolute
values are taken; Prop.~\ref{prop:min} records the unconditional one-sided
bound and the quantitative condition needed to exclude cancellation. The two
channels are modeled as the
finite-$N$ transition and extreme tail of a proposed common prolate-type
spectral edge (Section~\ref{ssec:twoforms}); convergence
$\nu_1\to\gamma_1$ is exactly $E\to0$. Its finite-precision measurement is the
matching-digit count
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
  \boxed{\;D(\lambda^2,N,P)=\min\!\bigl(D_{\mathrm{Wprec}}(P),\,
  D_{\mathrm{nModes}}(N,\lambda^2),\,D_{\mathrm{Primes}}(\lambda^2)\bigr)+O(1)\;}
\end{equation}
under the noncancellation condition of Prop.~\ref{prop:min}, and with an
unconditional lower bound in its absence. Here $D_{\mathrm{Wprec}}$ is the
instrument resolution alone. Since $D$ is the
relative error on a logarithmic scale, the law is a quantitative convergence
statement: $D\to\infty$ is precisely $\nu_1\to\gamma_1$, and the three budgets
are the independent mechanisms that drive (or stall) that convergence
(Prop.~\ref{prop:limit}). Six points
distinguish it from prior empirical fits:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The minimum is forced, not fitted} (Section~\ref{sec:skeleton}).
  In digit space $D$ is a negative logarithm of an error bounded by a sum of
  operationally independent channels, so the largest binds and $D$ is at least
  the minimum of the three per-source budgets, unconditionally, and equals it up
  to an $O(1)$ term absent cancellation (Prop.~\ref{prop:min}). This is the
  structural reason earlier single-formula digit fits failed. The precision budget is measurement, not
  mathematics: removing it leaves the single intrinsic
  edge~\eqref{eq:main-intrinsic}, whose two channels (modes, primes) are the only
  mathematical content.
\item \textbf{Each budget is analyzed from the construction}, not guessed. The
  precision budget follows from elementary round-off analysis; the measured
  prime ceiling is compared with the fourth-index prolate asymptotic, with the
  CCM-to-prolate transfer stated explicitly as open; and the mode budget is
  related to the analyticity of the Weil-form eigenvector.
\item \textbf{Every claim carries an explicit rigor level} (Table~\ref{tab:tiers}),
  separating the rigorously proved parts from those derived and validated
  against high-precision computation. We do not label the whole law a
  theorem; we state precisely which pieces are.
\item \textbf{It separates fixed-cutoff convergence from the growing-cutoff
  rate} (Prop.~\ref{prop:limit}). Under explicit simple/isolated/even
  hypotheses, the finite zero branch converges to a continuum-transform zero
  at fixed $\lambda^2$; if that limit differs from $\gamma_1$, increasing $N$
  and $P$ cannot make that branch converge to $\gamma_1$. The proposed
  growing-cutoff rate is a separate, explicitly conditional statement.
\item \textbf{It identifies the finite spectral reach of the truncation}
  (Section~\ref{sec:background}). The retained scaling lattice has the exact
  outer frequency $T_{\max}=2\pi N/\ln\lambda^2$. Combining this edge with the
  Riemann--von Mangoldt law gives a smooth estimate $K_{\max}^{(0)}$ for the
  number of zeta ordinates geometrically contained in the finite band. This is
  a capacity estimate, not a guarantee of accurate or correctly ordered
  recovery.
\item \textbf{No prior unified law, to our knowledge.} Earlier attempts are
  single closed-form fits of the digit count, and they fail: we tested two
  proposed rate laws against high-precision data and found them
  unsupported~\cite{AndrewsB}. Groskin~\cite{Groskin2026} likewise reports
  that no single smooth-convergence law fits his data and declines to assert a
  rate, and \'Sliwi\'nski~\cite{Sliwinski2026} establishes only a lower bound
  on the mean error. The unified characterization given here, the intrinsic
  two-channel edge~\eqref{eq:main-intrinsic} and its finite-precision
  measurement~\eqref{eq:main}, is, to the best of the author's knowledge, the
  first working closed-form convergence law for the construction. Its unity is
  the point: the data and classical asymptotics support a common spectral-edge
  hypothesis (Section~\ref{sec:equilibrium}) for the mode ramp and prime
  ceiling, which offers a mechanism for why a single expression can hold where
  earlier per-fits could not.
\end{enumerate}

The empirical claim has three distinct levels. First, the minimum-budget
structure is the exact bookkeeping skeleton. Second, the stretched-$\tanh$
family describes the observed mode ramps. Third, the particular closed map
$q_\gamma(\lambda^2)=2/(2\gamma+\ln\ln\lambda^2)$ together with
$c=4/\pi$ is a fixed operational submodel. The first two levels survive every
validation reported here. The third is accurate on the calibrated domain but
is now known not to extrapolate unchanged to $\lambda^2=1000$. Stating those
levels separately is essential: a measured failure of the large-cutoff
parameter map is a constraint on the formula, not a retraction of the
three-budget law or of the stretched-transition family.

The deepest new content is the mode budget $D_{\mathrm{nModes}}$
(Section~\ref{sec:nmodes}): the measured matching-digit ramp is the logarithm
of a stretched-exponential convergence, and its exponent is modeled by a sharp
coefficient-decay law for the eigenvector (Gevrey where $q\le1$). Proving that
regularity and its transfer to the
root is an explicit research program, not an end-to-end derivation. A
data-coordinate comparison explains how an eigenvalue-space local exponent of
the kind measured by Groskin~\cite{Groskin2026} can drift across a
stretched-exponential window; equality of the two asymptotic observables remains
conditional. Section~\ref{sec:equilibrium}
separates the rigorous Riemann--von Mangoldt counting backbone from the stronger
conjecture that the mode ramp and prime ceiling are two scales of one CCM
spectral edge.

\section{The construction and the object $D$}\label{sec:background}

We recall the construction of~\cite{CCM2025,CvS2025}. Let $\lambda>1$ and
$L=2\ln\lambda=\ln\lambda^2$. On $\mathcal H_\lambda=L^2([\lambda^{-1},\lambda],
du/u)$ the functions $V_n(u)=L^{-1/2}e^{2\pi i n\ln(\lambda u)/L}$ form an
orthonormal basis. The Weil quadratic form $QW_\lambda$, restricted to
$\mathrm{span}\{V_n:|n|\le N\}$, is a real symmetric $(2N{+}1)\times(2N{+}1)$
matrix whose archimedean part carries the local factor at infinity and whose
arithmetic part is a finite sum over prime powers $p^j\le\lambda^2$. Reflection
symmetry splits the matrix exactly into even and odd sectors. When the lowest
even eigenvalue is simple and lies below the lowest odd eigenvalue, the global
ground state $\xi$ is even; this is the critical finite-section hypothesis in
the CCM construction, not an identity valid without qualification. The
positive zeros of the associated Fourier--Mellin function $\widehat\xi$
then approximate the ordinates $\gamma_k$~\cite{CCM2025}.

The prime-power cutoff is likewise structural rather than a numerical
quadrature convention. Compact logarithmic support exhausts the arithmetic
translations beyond $p^j=\lambda^2$. As $u=\log\lambda^2$ varies, Groskin's
finite-path analysis proves that the matrix is continuous and piecewise real
analytic, with rank-one first-derivative jumps precisely at
$u=\log(p^j)$~\cite{GroskinMatrix2026}. Thus the arithmetic cutoff, basis
cutoff $N$, working precision, and any archimedean quadrature cutoff are four
distinct controls. Groskin's finite Guinand--Weil dictionary gives an exact
coefficient-vector-to-test-function translation and an explicit archimedean
tail budget~\cite{GroskinGW2026}; it therefore supplies an independent audit
of finite-cutoff matrix assembly, without by itself proving convergence of the
selected CCM zero.

\paragraph{Finite spectral reach and zero capacity.}
The retained scaling frequencies are $2\pi n/L$, $|n|\le N$, so their exact
outer edge is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tmax}
  \boxed{\;T_{\max}(N,\lambda^2)=\frac{2\pi N}{L}
  =\frac{2\pi N}{\ln\lambda^2}=\frac{N\pi}{\ln\lambda}.\;}
\end{equation}
This is a structural identity, not an empirical fit: it is the largest
unperturbed scaling frequency retained by $E_N$. It is already implicit in the
CCM definition $|t|\le N\pi/\ln\lambda$ and in the external carrier spectrum
of~\cite[Sec.~5.6]{CCM2025}; Groskin also writes the same band edge as
$\rho N$, with $\rho=2\pi/\ln\lambda^2$, in a different archimedean-tail
problem~\cite{GroskinGW2026}. We call it $T_{\max}$ only to make its role as the
finite truncation's natural spectral-reach boundary explicit.

Let $N_\zeta(T)$ count positive nontrivial zeta zeros through height $T$, with
multiplicity. The corresponding geometric zero capacity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kmax-exact}
  K_{\max}^{\mathrm{geom}}(N,\lambda^2):=N_\zeta(T_{\max}).
\end{equation}
The Riemann--von Mangoldt formula~\cite{Titchmarsh1986} gives
\[
  N_\zeta(T)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}
  -\frac{T}{2\pi}+\frac78+S(T)+O(T^{-1}),
\]
where $S(T)=\pi^{-1}\arg\zeta(\tfrac12+iT)$ in the standard convention,
and substitution of~\eqref{eq:tmax} therefore yields the smooth capacity
estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kmax}
  \boxed{\;K_{\max}^{(0)}(N,\lambda^2)
  =\frac{N}{\ln\lambda^2}
   \left[\log\!\left(\frac{N}{\ln\lambda^2}\right)-1\right]+\frac78.\;}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, geometric inclusion of the $k$th ordinate requires
$\gamma_k\le T_{\max}$, or
\begin{equation}\label{eq:k-reach-condition}
  N\ge\frac{\gamma_k\ln\lambda^2}{2\pi}.
\end{equation}
After a scan of the existing CCM/CvS and adjacent literature, no prior work
deriving $K_{\max}^{(0)}$ was found; to the author's knowledge, this particular
formula and its zero-capacity interpretation may be novel. The qualification
is important: $T_{\max}$ and $K_{\max}$ describe geometric availability in the
finite band. They do not prove that every included zero is recovered, positive,
correctly ordered, or accurate; those properties additionally depend on the
quality of the finite eigenstate and are not claimed here.

Two facts about $\widehat\xi$ are used below: $\widehat\xi$ is entire, and (in
the critical case) all its zeros are real and coincide with the spectrum. Both
are proven \emph{self-contained} in~\cite{CCM2025}: the closed form of the
transform is \cite[Prop.~5.9]{CCM2025} (rederived from scratch in
Appendix~\ref{app:selfcontained}), and entirety/reality is
\cite[Thm.~5.10]{CCM2025}, whose proof chain (Lemmas~5.2 and~5.4 there: the
matrix structure $\tau_{ij}=(b_i-b_j)/(i-j)$, an elementary commutator
identity, and self-adjointness in the induced inner product) is complete
within~\cite{CCM2025}. The Carath\'eodory--Fej\'er--type reality mechanism
\emph{originates} in the Connes--van Suijlekom paper~\cite{CvS2025}, which we
cite as the historical source; no statement used in this paper depends on it
(Appendix~\ref{app:selfcontained}). An independent classical foundation for the
truncated Weil form predates both: Weil's positivity criterion, Yoshida's
unconditional positivity theorem for test functions of sufficiently small
support~\cite{Yoshida1992}, Bombieri's study of the truncated quadratic form
and the eigenvalues of its finite sections~\cite{Bombieri2000}, and Suzuki's
screw-function framework~\cite{SuzukiJLMS,SuzukiHilbert,Suzuki2026}, which
unifies all of these by means of continuous functions.

For the configurations used in the reported above-floor analysis, the selected
eigenvector $\xi$ is even and the smallest eigenvalue is simple, with the
even-symmetry verified
numerically across $38$ configurations spanning $\lambda^2=13$--$1000$
in~\cite{AndrewsA}.

The object of study, $D(\lambda^2,N,P)$, is a digit count: $D=-\log_{10}
(\text{relative error})$. The relative error itself,
$E=|\nu_1-\gamma_1|/|\gamma_1|$, is the intrinsic object of the
law~\eqref{eq:main-intrinsic}; $D$ is its measurement in digits, and the
arithmetic of that measurement is what makes the minimum structure
of~\eqref{eq:main} inevitable.

\section{The three-budget skeleton}\label{sec:skeleton}

The computed approximation $\nu_1$ carries error from three sources:
finite working precision (round-off), finite basis size (mode truncation), and
the displacement of the fixed-cutoff continuum limit. We now \emph{define} the
channels, rather than posit their additivity. At a cutoff satisfying the
spectral hypotheses of Section~\ref{ssec:criterion}, write
$\nu_1(N,\lambda^2)$ for the selected positive zero branch of the exact
(infinite-precision) finite-$N$ operator and
$\nu_1(\infty,\lambda^2)$ for its $N\to\infty$ limit, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:channels}
  \varepsilon_{\mathrm{modes}}(N,\lambda^2)
   :=\frac{|\nu_1(N,\lambda^2)-\nu_1(\infty,\lambda^2)|}{|\gamma_1|},
  \qquad
  \varepsilon_{\mathrm{primes}}(\lambda^2)
   :=\frac{|\nu_1(\infty,\lambda^2)-\gamma_1|}{|\gamma_1|},
\end{equation}
the finite-$N$ displacement at fixed cutoff and the cutoff displacement at
infinite basis size, with $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{round}}(P)$ the round-off
perturbation of the computed $\nu_1$ relative to the exact finite-$N$ value.
The triangle inequality then gives, for the computed relative error,
\[
  E \;\le\; \varepsilon_{\mathrm{round}}(P)
  +\varepsilon_{\mathrm{modes}}(N,\lambda^2)+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{primes}}(\lambda^2),
\]
an inequality, not an identity: the signed displacements can in principle
cancel. The channels are \emph{independent} in a precise operational (not
probabilistic or structural) sense: each answers to its own parameter
(round-off to $P$, truncation to $N$, prime content to $\lambda^2$), so any
one can be driven below the others at will. (Structurally they are coupled:
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{modes}}$ depends on $\lambda^2$ through both the exponent
$q$ and the Shannon coordinate of Section~\ref{sec:nmodes}, and below the
precision floor round-off corrupts the eigenvector nonlinearly,
Section~\ref{ssec:evenness}; the operational sense is all
Prop.~\ref{prop:min} uses.)

\begin{proposition}[The minimum is forced]\label{prop:min}
Let $\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2,\varepsilon_3\ge0$ and let $E\ge0$ satisfy
$E\le\Sigma:=\sum_i\varepsilon_i$. Then, unconditionally,
\[
  D:=-\log_{10}E\;\ge\;-\log_{10}\Sigma
  \;\ge\;\min_i\bigl(-\log_{10}\varepsilon_i\bigr)-\log_{10}3 ,
\]
since $\Sigma\le 3\max_i\varepsilon_i$. If in addition no cancellation occurs
--- quantitatively, $E\ge\theta\max_i\varepsilon_i$ for some fixed
$\theta\in(0,1]$ --- then also
$D\le\min_i(-\log_{10}\varepsilon_i)+\log_{10}(1/\theta)$, whence
$D=\min(D_{\mathrm{Wprec}},D_{\mathrm{nModes}},D_{\mathrm{Primes}})+O(1)$.
\end{proposition}

The lower bound, that the computed digit count is at least the binding budget
minus $\log_{10}3<0.48$ of a digit, is a rigorous consequence of the triangle
inequality alone. The matching upper bound requires the explicit
noncancellation condition $E\ge\theta\max_i\varepsilon_i$. Its additive
constant is $\log_{10}(1/\theta)$; it is not bounded by $\log_{10}3$ without an
independent lower bound on $\theta$. Cancellation is most plausible near
crossover seams, where two channels have comparable magnitude, but the
triangle inequality alone neither confines it to those seams nor excludes an
isolated cancellation elsewhere. Empirically, no seam in the recorded
starvation sweeps shows $D$ exceeding the binding minimum by more than the
fitted bounded offset. Accordingly, the equality form used below is
a quantitatively testable noncancellation model, while the one-sided bound is
unconditional. Its content is that the \emph{binding mechanism} changes across
the parameter space (round-off, mode truncation, then prime content), not that
$D$ is literally nonsmooth (the negative logarithm of a signed near-sum can be
smooth and can also exhibit cancellation spikes).

This is why, in digit space, $D$ is a minimum rather than a sum or product, and
why global single-expression fits of the digit count fail in practice: a single
elementary term cannot simultaneously hold a hard precision wall, a prime
ceiling, and a $\lambda^2$-coupled mode ramp. In the intrinsic form the error
is, by contrast, the two-channel decomposition~\eqref{eq:main-intrinsic}; the
digit-space minimum
is what the negative logarithm of that near-sum becomes, the largest term
binding. The junctions differ in character.
The precision junction is a hard wall, since round-off is a clamp, so its corner
is sharp. The mode$\to$prime junction, by contrast, is observed as a wide,
smooth saturation (the $\tanh$ of Section~\ref{sec:nmodes}). The measured digit
count approaches the fixed-cutoff limit as $N\to\infty$ along the selected
branch. Neither a pointwise inequality
$D_{\mathrm{nModes}}\le D_{\mathrm{Primes}}$ nor monotone root accuracy is
asserted: finite-$N$ displacement can partially cancel the cutoff displacement.

\paragraph{The starvation protocol.}
Each budget is isolated experimentally by \emph{starvation}: to read $D_i$
directly, set the other two parameters large enough that their budgets exceed
$D_i$, so the channel of interest dominates $E$ outright and the cancellation
caveat of Prop.~\ref{prop:min} is vacuous. This protocol is the
experimental mirror of the minimum structure. It is what makes the digit form
falsifiable rather than descriptive, since each budget's functional form is
measured in a regime where it alone binds. We use it throughout
Sections~\ref{sec:wprec}--\ref{sec:primes}.

\paragraph{Validity premise for empirical calibration.}
The three budgets begin after the finite CCM matrix has been assembled
correctly. Matrix-assembly error is not a fourth intrinsic convergence
mechanism and is not added to the caller-facing law. It is instead an
admissibility condition on calibration and validation data. Deep spectra can
be stable under increased arithmetic precision while still being wrong
because of an inadequate archimedean cutoff or a precision-stable special
function defect. Accordingly, a state enters the empirical archive only when
its assembly is supported by an independent cutoff or closed-form check,
structural symmetry tests, residual verification, and recorded reference-zero
provenance. This follows the verified-computation principle that numerical
work should check the hypotheses of an analytic bound rather than infer
correctness from a small residual alone~\cite{Rump2010}; the finite
Guinand--Weil tail analysis~\cite{GroskinGW2026} and the independent
quadrature and closed-form audits~\cite{AndradeQuadrature2026,AndradeClosedForm2026}
make the requirement concrete for the present matrix family.

\subsection{The two forms of the law}\label{ssec:twoforms}

Of the three budgets, only two (mode truncation and prime cutoff) are
properties of the finite operator; round-off is a property of the computation.
Separating them gives the two forms stated in Section~\ref{sec:intro}.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Intrinsic (spectral) form.} The mathematical content is the
two-channel decomposition~\eqref{eq:main-intrinsic}, with
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{modes}}\sim
\exp(-aN^{q})\to0$ as $N\to\infty$ (Section~\ref{sec:nmodes}) and
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{primes}}\to0$ as $\lambda^2\to\infty$
(Section~\ref{sec:primes}). It makes no reference to precision. Under
$(\mathrm{H}_\lambda^\ast)$, the mode displacement from the selected
fixed-cutoff continuum zero vanishes as $N\to\infty$; the decay of the
continuum-zero displacement as $\lambda^2\to\infty$ is the separate
growing-cutoff premise tested in Section~\ref{sec:primes}. Vanishing of both
channels is sufficient for $E\to0$. The converse is not automatic for signed
errors because finite-section and cutoff displacements may cancel. At a fixed
cutoff satisfying $(\mathrm{H}_\lambda^\ast)$, however, the selected branch
converges to $\nu_\infty(\lambda)\ne\gamma_1$, so increasing $N$ alone cannot
make that branch converge to $\gamma_1$ (Prop.~\ref{prop:limit}).

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Observed (digit) form.} A computation with caller-requested target
$P=P_{\mathrm{req}}$, realized internally at $P_{\mathrm{work}}$, resolves $E$ only down to its round-off floor, so the measured matching-digit
count is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:observed}
  D(\lambda^2,N,P)=\min\!\bigl(D_{\mathrm{Wprec}}(P),\,-\log_{10}E(N,\lambda^2)\bigr)
   =\min\!\bigl(D_{\mathrm{Wprec}},D_{\mathrm{nModes}},D_{\mathrm{Primes}}\bigr)+O(1),
\end{equation}
where the second equality is conditional on a parameter-uniform
noncancellation bound as in Prop.~\ref{prop:min}. Here $D_{\mathrm{Wprec}}(P)$
is the instrument resolution: it is no property of $\nu_1$, $\gamma_1$, or the
operator, and as $P\to\infty$ it disappears, returning the intrinsic form in
digit units. Thus the boxed law~\eqref{eq:main} is the finite-precision
\emph{measurement} of the intrinsic law~\eqref{eq:main-intrinsic}.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{One-edge parametrization.} The data admit a useful common-edge
parametrization of the mode and prime channels. Dividing out the ceiling
(Section~\ref{sec:nmodes}) writes $D_{\mathrm{nModes}}=D_{\mathrm{Primes}}\cdot g$
with $g=\tanh\!\bigl((c\,N/(\lambda^2\ln\lambda^2))^{q}\bigr)\uparrow1$ as
$N\to\infty$, so $D_{\mathrm{Primes}}=\lim_{N\to\infty}D_{\mathrm{nModes}}$ is the
$N\to\infty$ ceiling of the mode ramp in this parametrization; equivalently the
digit law can be written using $D_{\mathrm{edge}}(N,\lambda^2)=
D_{\mathrm{Primes}}\cdot g$ capped by precision,
$D=\min(D_{\mathrm{Wprec}},D_{\mathrm{edge}})+O(1)$. Algebraic success of this
parametrization does not prove that both errors arise from one spectral object.
The Landau--Widom transition and Fuchs tail provide the evidence for that
stronger common-edge hypothesis, formulated with its required comparison
theorems in Section~\ref{sec:equilibrium}.

\section{The precision budget $D_{\mathrm{Wprec}}$}\label{sec:wprec}

The caller specifies a decimal target $P=P_{\mathrm{req}}$. The implementation
converts that target to an internal precision $P_{\mathrm{work}}$ by allocating
$\lceil P_{\mathrm{req}}\ln10/\ln2\rceil+64$ bits; the guard reserve contributes
approximately $19.27$ decimal digits before accumulation and conditioning
losses. For fixed $(\lambda^2,N)$, a backward-stable computation has unit
round-off proportional to $10^{-P_{\mathrm{work}}}$. Its forward root error is
bounded by an effective condition factor $\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}$ that includes
matrix assembly, separation of the selected eigenpair, transform evaluation,
and the inverse slope at the selected simple zero. If these factors and the
algorithmic backward-error constant remain independent of the requested target,
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wprec}
  D_{\mathrm{Wprec}}(P_{\mathrm{req}})
  =P_{\mathrm{req}}-\log_{10}\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}+O(1)
  =P_{\mathrm{req}}+O(1),
\end{equation}
where the guard reserve is absorbed in the implementation-dependent constant.
This is a fixed-problem stability statement, not a claim that the condition
factor is uniform as $N$ or $\lambda^2$ grows. At $\lambda^2=13$ and
$N=80$, the validation sweep $P\in\{30,45,90,180\}$ gives
$D\in\{49.238,55.654,55.654,55.654\}$, exhibiting the switch from the
working-precision budget to the fixed-cutoff prime ceiling. Isolating
this budget at $\lambda^2=50$ and $N=400$, a sweep over
$P\in\{50,100,150,200\}$ gives an endpoint slope of $0.99970$ and an ordinary
least-squares slope of $1.00205$ with $R^2=0.99994$, consistent with the
predicted unit precision slope. The exact
offset is not a property of the construction and we do not assign it physical
meaning. \emph{Status: the unit slope is T1 conditional on a fixed,
backward-stable problem with a simple selected root; backward stability of the
complete implementation is supported by the precision sweep and independent
replay checks, not proved here.}

\subsection{Symmetry, positivity, and the precision floor}\label{ssec:evenness}

A question the precision floor settles, and one easy to answer wrongly
without it, is whether the even symmetry of the ground eigenvector, and the
positivity of $\varepsilon_N$, are genuine features of the operator or merely of
the regime above the floor. The CCM construction~\cite{CCM2025} posits a simple
smallest eigenvalue with an \emph{even} eigenvector (its Step~1) and enforces
this with a forced-even projection. The reproduction of~\cite{AndrewsA} tested
that projection directly: across $38$ configurations ($\lambda^2=13$--$1000$,
$N=10$--$800$, spanning both sides of the precision floor) with the projection
\emph{disabled}, the natural smallest eigenvector converged to the same even
state produced by the projection, with numerically equivalent eigenvalues,
at every tested configuration above the floor. The forced-even projection is
thus empirically a no-op over that tested range: even symmetry is exhibited by
the unprojected finite operator there, not manufactured by the projection.

The qualifier ``above the floor'' is essential, and it is precisely where the
budget of this section bites. When the target eigenvalue lies below
$D_{\mathrm{Wprec}}$, smaller than the round-off that the working precision
can resolve, the computed eigenvector is dominated by numerical noise: it can
present spurious mixed symmetry, and the smallest computed eigenvalue can carry
a spurious sign. These are artifacts of precision starvation, not properties of
the operator; raising $P$ past the floor dissolves them, and the natural
eigenvector is again even, into the deep near-zero spectrum. (This is also the
regime in which the round-off channel of Section~\ref{sec:skeleton} stops
acting as an additive perturbation of $\nu_1$: below the floor it corrupts the
eigenvector nonlinearly, so the decomposition~\eqref{eq:channels}, like the
symmetry and positivity statements of this subsection, is an above-floor
statement.)

This even, positive ground state was documented in our reproduction~\cite{AndrewsA}.
In subsequent correspondence, and in a revision of his own paper~\cite{Groskin2026},
Groskin independently corroborated it by a different route: the sign changes his
separate quadrature implementation had shown among the deepest eigenvalues at a
finite archimedean cutoff $T$ were identified as a finite-$T$ artifact that
vanishes as $T$ is increased, consistent with the naturally even, positive ground
state of~\cite{AndrewsA}. Working precision $P$ and archimedean cutoff $T$ are
therefore independent controls: a deep-spectrum value is trustworthy only when it
is stable across two settings of \emph{each}, not when it merely holds steady in
one. Across the tested and independently corroborated configurations, the even,
positive ground state persists into the deep near-zero spectrum, while observed
breakdowns disappear when the relevant precision or cutoff is raised. A uniform
statement for the full growing-cutoff path remains the sector-gap problem of
Section~\ref{sec:rigor}.

\section{The mode budget $D_{\mathrm{nModes}}$}\label{sec:nmodes}

This is the convergence rate in $N$, the open problem of~\cite{CCM2025}. In
the intrinsic form it is the mode channel $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{modes}}$; as a
digit count it is the rising transition in the proposed common-edge
parametrization (Section~\ref{sec:equilibrium}), climbing toward the prime
ceiling.
Dividing out that ceiling (Section~\ref{sec:primes}), write the ramp ratio
$g=D_{\mathrm{nModes}}/D_{\mathrm{Primes}}\in[0,1]$. On the validated
closed-submodel domain $7\le\lambda^2\le100$ we use
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nmodes}
\begin{aligned}
  D_{\mathrm{nModes}}(N,\lambda^2)
    &=D_{\mathrm{Primes}}(\lambda^2)\,
      \tanh\!\Bigl(\bigl(cN/(\lambda^2\ln\lambda^2)\bigr)^{q_\gamma(\lambda^2)}\Bigr),\\
  q_\gamma(\lambda^2)&=\frac{2}{2\gamma+\ln\ln\lambda^2},
  \qquad c=\frac4\pi,\qquad 7\le\lambda^2\le100.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The stretched-$\tanh$ family and the coordinate
$\lambda^2\ln\lambda^2$ are tested more broadly; the statement above is the
fixed caller-facing parameter map, not a claim that $q_\gamma$ and $c$ persist
unchanged at arbitrary cutoff. We build the model in four steps: the
stretched-exponential meaning of the ramp
(\ref{ssec:stretch}), the candidate archimedean rate (\ref{ssec:arch}), the prime
correction giving $q$ (\ref{ssec:prime}), and the Shannon coordinate
(\ref{ssec:coord}).

\subsection{The ramp is a stretched exponential}\label{ssec:stretch}

\begin{proposition}[Stretched-exponential reparametrization]\label{prop:stretched}
Suppose the pre-saturation mode budget has the asymptotic form
\[
 D_{\mathrm{nModes}}(N,\lambda^2)
 =\frac{a}{\ln 10}N^q+r(N),
 \qquad r(N)=o(N^q),
\]
for some $a=a(\lambda^2)>0$ and exponent $q=q(\lambda^2)$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{(Stretched-exponential.)} The finite-section root error obeys
  \[
  \varepsilon_{\mathrm{modes}}(N,\lambda^2)
  =\exp\!\bigl(-aN^q+o(N^q)\bigr).
  \]
\item[(ii)] \emph{(Local power-law coordinate.)} If $r$ is differentiable as a
function of $\ln N$ and $d r/d\ln N=o(N^q)$, then the logarithmic local
power-law exponent satisfies
  \[
  2s_{\mathrm{loc}}(N)
  :=-\frac{d\ln\varepsilon_{\mathrm{modes}}}{d\ln N}
  =a\,q\,N^q+o(N^q).
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Part (i) follows from
$\ln\varepsilon_{\mathrm{modes}}=-(\ln10)D_{\mathrm{nModes}}
=-aN^q+o(N^q)$. Differentiating this identity with respect to $\ln N$
and using the stated derivative control on $r$ gives part (ii).
\end{proof}

The proposition is an exact change of coordinates for the measured root
error; by itself it does not identify an eigenvector regularity class. The
proposed mechanism is sharper: the same exponent is conjectured to govern the
phase-aligned eigenvector tail. In standard terminology this is the
\emph{Gevrey scale}: the
Gevrey class $G^{\mu}$, $\mu\ge1$, is characterized by Fourier-coefficient
decay $\exp(-c\,|k|^{1/\mu})$, reducing to strip-analytic functions at $\mu=1$
\cite{Rodino1993,ToftNabizadeh2017}. Thus for $0<q\le1$ the statement
$|\xi_k|\sim\exp(-c\,k^{q})$ is the sharp statement
$\xi\in G^{1/q}$ (and no better). When a finite-range fit gives $q>1$, as at
$\lambda^2=7$ in Table~\ref{tab:q}, it instead describes an ultra-analytic
coefficient-decay scale; calling that ordinary Gevrey regularity would be
incorrect. Establishing the coefficient
law, and then transferring it to the transform zero, are separate open steps.
The open ``variance-to-exponent
map'' of Section~\ref{ssec:prime} is thereby a precisely posed regularity
lemma on a classical scale: \emph{prove the sharp coefficient-decay class with
exponent $q(\lambda^2)$ and, where $q\le1$, membership in
$G^{1/q(\lambda^2)}$, with
$1/q=\gamma+\tfrac12\ln\ln\lambda^2$}. The measured exponents cross the
analytic value $q=1$ near $\lambda^2=13$ and then decrease with $\lambda^2$
(Table~\ref{tab:q}). This is consistent with a transition from ultra-analytic
finite-range decay at the smallest cutoff to a prime-roughened Gevrey scale,
but that regularity interpretation remains conjectural.

The expanded eigenstate archive supplies a direct stress test of this
interpretation. Applying one fixed fractional-window fit to the stored
coefficient envelopes does not recover a cutoff-only exponent: at
$\lambda^2=250$, for example, the effective exponent falls from about $1.72$
at $N=60$ to about $0.99$ at $N=1500$, and it also moves when the fitted part
of the finite vector is changed. This does not refute a sharp law for the
continuum eigenvector, because the available vectors are finite sections and
their edges move with $N$. It does rule out treating the exponent fitted from
the root-error ramp as an already measured coefficient exponent. A promotion
of the conjecture must therefore prove a fitting-window-independent continuum
tail statement and a separate tail-to-root transfer; finite coefficient plots
alone are insufficient.

The force of part~(i) is that it converts the open problem of \emph{measuring}
a convergence rate into the analytic question of \emph{explaining} the
observed exponent: the single exponent $q$ fixes the entire pre-saturation
curve. It also explains why a fixed power-law fit drifts across an actually
stretched-exponential window and resists extrapolation
(Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

Part~(ii) gives a direct data-level reconciliation with the local exponent
$s(c)$ measured by Groskin~\cite{Groskin2026}, provided the two experiments are
sampling the same asymptotic error channel; this comparison is taken up in
Section~\ref{sec:discussion}. Separately, for a simple isolated eigenvalue the
Rayleigh-quotient error is second order in the phase-aligned eigenvector error,
$|\varepsilon-\varepsilon_N|=O(\|\xi-\xi_N\|^2)$
\cite{BabuskaOsborn1991,ZhuArgentatiKnyazev2013,Nakatsukasa2018,HaasNakatsukasa2025}. That result concerns the Rayleigh eigenvalue. It does
not by itself square the displacement of a zero of $\widehat\xi_N$; the latter
requires the quantitative tail-to-transform estimates and the simple-zero
perturbation mechanism stated below. \emph{Status: the reparametrization and
local-slope identities are T1; their eigenvector-regularity and cross-observable
interpretations remain conditional.}

\subsection{The candidate archimedean rate $\delta=\pi/4$}\label{ssec:arch}

The exact archimedean input is the completed real Gamma factor
$\Gamma_\mathbb{R}(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)$. Its logarithmic derivative
$\psi(\tfrac14+\tfrac{iz}{2})\sim\log(z/2)$ supplies the large-frequency
$\log|z|$ symbol identified by Suzuki~\cite{Suzuki2026}, while Stirling gives
the real-axis envelope
$|\Gamma(\tfrac14+\tfrac{iz}{2})|\asymp
|z|^{-1/4}\exp(-\pi|z|/4)$. Thus the exact archimedean envelope rate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta}
  \delta=\pi/4 .
\end{equation}

The same value follows differentially. On the critical line the archimedean
log-derivative has the large-$z$ expansion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:digamma-split}
  \psi\!\left(\tfrac14+\tfrac{iz}{2}\right)=\log\frac{z}{2}
  +i\,\frac{\pi}{2}+o(1),\qquad z\to+\infty,
\end{equation}
since the argument $\tfrac14+\tfrac{iz}{2}$ runs in the imaginary
(critical-line) direction, so $\arg\bigl(\tfrac14+\tfrac{iz}{2}\bigr)\to\pi/2$.
Its real and imaginary parts carry complementary information:
\begin{itemize}
\item the \emph{real} part $\operatorname{Re}\psi(\tfrac14+\tfrac{iz}{2})\to
  \log(z/2)$ is the large-frequency symbol, Suzuki's $\log|z|$
  multiplier~\cite{Suzuki2026};
\item the \emph{imaginary} part $\operatorname{Im}\psi(\tfrac14+\tfrac{iz}{2})\to
  \tfrac{\pi}{2}$ sets the envelope, through
  \[
    \frac{d}{dz}\log\bigl|\Gamma_\mathbb{R}(\tfrac12+iz)\bigr|
    =-\tfrac12\operatorname{Im}\psi\!\left(\tfrac14+\tfrac{iz}{2}\right)
    \longrightarrow-\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac{\pi}{2}=-\tfrac{\pi}{4}.
  \]
\end{itemize}
Both statements arise from the one Gamma factor. The elementary identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pi4}
  \delta=\frac{\pi}{4}=\frac12\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}=\tfrac12\arg(i),
\end{equation}
records the half-angle generated by the critical-line direction. It is an
envelope identity, not a proof of an analyticity strip for the finite CCM
ground state.

Two transfer steps remain open. First, the finite ground-state transform, which
is of exponential type $L/2$, is not literally the completed xi function; one
must prove that its phase-aligned coefficient tail inherits the
$\Gamma_\mathbb{R}$ envelope in the relevant growing window. Second, one must
prove how that tail controls the selected transform-zero displacement, including
whether the observed exponent is doubled. The measured finite coefficient rate
($\approx0.71$ at $\lambda^2=100$) is therefore a separate, prime-modulated
quantity rather than an instance of a proved $\pi/4$ law. The exact Gamma
calculation identifies a compelling candidate value for the open realization
step; it does not close that step. Conditional on global RH,
Section~\ref{ssec:gramdd} identifies the finite eigenvalue-to-root transfer as
a first-order divided-difference identity. That result settles the form of the
root-transfer mechanism but not the fixed-cutoff convergence rate, which still
requires the Galerkin estimates and limiting norming control stated below.

\begin{lemma}[Lattice sampling of the coefficients]\label{lem:sampling}
The Fourier--Mellin transform of the $N$-mode eigenvector has the closed
form $\widehat\xi_N(z)=2L^{-1/2}\sin(zL/2)\sum_{|j|\le N}\xi_j/(z-2\pi j/L)$
(\cite[Prop.~5.9]{CCM2025}; rederived from scratch in
Appendix~\ref{app:selfcontained}). On the frequency lattice $z_k=2\pi k/L$,
\[
  \widehat\xi_N(2\pi k/L)=(-1)^k\sqrt{L}\,\xi_k\ \ (|k|\le N),\qquad
  \widehat\xi_N(2\pi k/L)=0\ \ (|k|>N),
\]
so $|\xi_k|=L^{-1/2}\,\bigl|\widehat\xi_N(2\pi k/L)\bigr|$, with
$L=\ln\lambda^2$ the period of the basis $\{V_n\}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At $z_k=2\pi k/L$ the factor $\sin(zL/2)=(-1)^k\sin\!\bigl((z-z_k)L/2\bigr)$
has a simple zero, so every $j\ne k$ term vanishes and the $j=k$ pole is
removable:
$\lim_{z\to z_k}2L^{-1/2}(-1)^k\sin\!\bigl((z-z_k)L/2\bigr)/(z-z_k)
=(-1)^k\sqrt{L}$. For $|k|>N$ there is no $j=k$ term and the common factor
$\sin(z_kL/2)=0$.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:sampling} rigorously fixes the lattice spacing $2\pi/L$. If the
coefficient tail inherits the candidate Gamma envelope,
$\exp(-\delta(2\pi/L)N)$, and if the selected-zero displacement has the
observed doubled exponent, then the leading digit slope is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slope}
  \frac{\partial D_{\mathrm{nModes}}}{\partial N}
  =\frac{2\delta\,(2\pi/L)}{\ln10}=\frac{\pi^2}{\ln10\;\ln\lambda^2}
  \approx\frac{4.286}{\ln\lambda^2},
\end{equation}
a parameter-free conditional prediction. At $\lambda^2=7$, where few primes enter and
$q\approx1$, \eqref{eq:slope} gives $2.203$ against a measured small-$N$ slope
of $2.264$, agreement to $3\%$ with no free parameters. At larger $\lambda^2$
the measured early slope runs above~\eqref{eq:slope} exactly as the $q<1$
concavity (below) requires.

What \emph{is} rigorous is the final transfer from a locally uniform transform
estimate to displacement of a simple zero. We record it next. The open content
before that step includes the coefficient law, quantitative Galerkin transfer,
and the observed doubling.

\begin{lemma}[Simple-zero displacement under uniform convergence]\label{lem:rouche}
Let $a\in\mathbb C$, and let $F$ be holomorphic on a neighborhood of the closed
disc $\overline{D}(a,r)$, with a simple zero at $a$ and no other zero in the
disc. Shrink $r$ if necessary so that
$|F(z)|\ge\tfrac12|F'(a)|\,|z-a|$ throughout the disc; this follows by applying
continuity to the nonvanishing holomorphic quotient $F(z)/(z-a)$, extended at
$a$ by $F'(a)$. Let $G$ be holomorphic on the disc and set
$\eta:=\sup_{\overline{D}(a,r)}|G-F|$. If
$\rho:=2\eta/|F'(a)|<r$, then $G$ has exactly one zero $b$ in $D(a,r)$, and
\[
  |b-a|\;\le\;\frac{2\,\eta}{|F'(a)|}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $\eta=0$, the conclusion is immediate. Otherwise, on $|z-a|=r$ the
hypothesis $\rho<r$ gives $|F(z)|>\eta\ge|G(z)-F(z)|$, so Rouch\'e's theorem
gives $G$ exactly one zero $b$ in $D(a,r)$. For every $s$ with
$\rho<s<r$, the same strict inequality holds on $|z-a|=s$. Thus $b\in D(a,s)$
for every such $s$, and letting $s\downarrow\rho$ gives $|b-a|\le\rho$.
\end{proof}

At fixed cutoff, assume $\widehat\xi_\infty$ has a simple isolated zero
$\nu_\infty(\lambda)$ as in~$(\mathrm{H}_\lambda^\ast)$. Applied with
$a=\nu_\infty(\lambda)$, $F=\widehat\xi_\infty$, and
$G=\widehat\xi_N$, the lemma converts uniform convergence near the continuum
zero into finite-section zero displacement with the explicit constant
$2/|\widehat\xi_\infty'(\nu_\infty)|$. It does not, by itself, compare
$\nu_\infty(\lambda)$ with the zeta ordinate $\gamma_1$.

A second, high-value transfer runs from the coefficient tail to the transform.
The finite-section eigenvector is not the truncation of the limit eigenvector,
so the transform difference carries both a discarded-tail term and a retained
Galerkin error. We preserve the complete route while making its quantitative
Galerkin input explicit.

\begin{lemma}[Tail-to-transform transfer under quantitative Galerkin
stability]\label{lem:tail}
Fix $\lambda^2$, assume the ground state of the continuum form $QW_\lambda$ is
simple with spectral gap $g(\lambda^2)>0$, assume
$\widehat\xi_\infty$ has a simple isolated zero $\nu_\infty(\lambda)$, and
suppose the limit eigenvector satisfies
$|\xi^{(\infty)}_j|\le C\exp(-c\,j^{q})$ for some $c>0$, $q>0$.
Let $r>0$ be small enough that $\overline{D}(\nu_\infty,r)$ contains no other
zero of $\widehat\xi_\infty$ and has positive distance
$d(\lambda^2,r)$ from the frequency lattice $\{2\pi j/L\}$. Write
$P_N$ for projection onto $\operatorname{span}\{V_j:|j|\le N\}$,
$Q_N=I-P_N$, and $QW_\lambda^N=P_NQW_\lambda P_N$. In addition, assume the
following quantitative Galerkin estimates hold for some fixed $m,s\ge0$ and
all sufficiently large $N$:
\begin{equation*}\label{eq:qgalerkin}
\begin{aligned}
 \|P_NQW_\lambda Q_N\xi^{(\infty)}\|_2
   &\le C_1N^m e^{-cN^q},\\
 \operatorname{dist}\!\left(
   \varepsilon^\infty,
   \sigma(QW_\lambda^N)\setminus\{\varepsilon_N\}\right)
   &\ge \tfrac12 g(\lambda^2),\\
 \sup_{z\in\overline D(\nu_\infty,r)}|\widehat T[e](z)|
   &\le C_2N^s\|e\|_2,
   \qquad e\in\operatorname{ran}P_N.
\end{aligned}
\tag{$\mathrm{QG}_\lambda$}
\end{equation*}
Normalize the eigenvectors and fix phases by
$\langle\xi^{(N)},\xi^{(\infty)}\rangle\ge0$. Then
\[
  \sup_{|z-\nu_\infty|\le r}\bigl|\widehat\xi_N(z)-\widehat\xi_\infty(z)\bigr|
  \;\le\;\exp\!\bigl(-cN^{q}+o(N^q)\bigr),\qquad N\to\infty .
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
With $\ell^1$ coefficients the closed form of Lemma~\ref{lem:sampling}
extends to the limit: the series
$\widehat\xi_\infty(z)=2L^{-1/2}\sin(zL/2)\sum_j\xi_j/(z-2\pi j/L)$ converges
locally uniformly (lattice poles removable as before) and defines an entire
function. Split
$\widehat\xi_N-\widehat\xi_\infty=\widehat{T}[\xi^{(N)}-P_N\xi^{(\infty)}]
-\widehat{T}[Q_N\xi^{(\infty)}]$, with $\widehat T$ the linear
coefficients-to-function map. \emph{Tail piece:} on the disc
$|\sin(zL/2)|\le\cosh(rL/2)$ and $|z-2\pi j/L|\ge\pi|j|/L$ for $|j|$ large,
so $|\widehat T[Q_N\xi^{(\infty)}]|\le
C_3\sum_{|j|>N}e^{-cj^{q}}/|j|\le C_4N^{-q}e^{-cN^{q}}(1+o(1))$, by comparison
with $\int_N^\infty e^{-ct^{q}}t^{-1}\,dt$. \emph{Head piece:} the Galerkin residual
collapses to the tail,
\[
  \bigl(QW^N_\lambda-\varepsilon^\infty\bigr)\bigl(P_N\xi^{(\infty)}\bigr)
  =P_NQW_\lambda\xi^{(\infty)}-P_NQW_\lambda Q_N\xi^{(\infty)}
   -\varepsilon^\infty P_N\xi^{(\infty)}
  =-P_NQW_\lambda Q_N\xi^{(\infty)},
\]
using $QW_\lambda\xi^{(\infty)}=\varepsilon^\infty\xi^{(\infty)}$.
Let $\Pi_N$ be the spectral projection onto the normalized finite ground state
$\xi^{(N)}$. Expanding $P_N\xi^{(\infty)}$ in an orthonormal eigenbasis of
$QW_\lambda^N$ and using the spectral-separation line of
eq.~\eqref{eq:qgalerkin} gives
\[
 \|(I-\Pi_N)P_N\xi^{(\infty)}\|_2
 \le \frac{2}{g(\lambda^2)}
      \|P_NQW_\lambda Q_N\xi^{(\infty)}\|_2.
\]
After phase alignment and normalization, the first line of
eq.~\eqref{eq:qgalerkin}, together with the exponentially small coefficient
tail, therefore gives
$\|\xi^{(N)}-P_N\xi^{(\infty)}\|_2
\le\operatorname{poly}(N)e^{-cN^q}$. The transform-evaluation line controls
the head piece, while the direct series estimate controls the tail piece.
All polynomial factors contribute only $o(N^q)$ in the exponent.
\end{proof}

The estimates $(\mathrm{QG}_\lambda)$ are a concrete research target, not a
consequence of the lowest-Rayleigh-value convergence in
\cite[Prop.~3.4]{CCM2025}. A route to proving them is to establish explicit
archimedean and prime matrix-tail bounds, verify that the Fourier subspaces are
a form core with compact-resolvent spectral approximation, prove convergence
of the first two eigenvalues (hence persistence of the gap), and then apply a
quantitative spectral-projection estimate of the type developed in
\cite{BabuskaOsborn1991}. The lemma is retained because it identifies exactly
how those operator estimates would convert coefficient decay into root
convergence without discarding the mechanism.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:single}
Under the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:tail},
$|\nu_1(N,\lambda^2)-\nu_1(\infty,\lambda^2)|\le\exp(-c\,N^{q}(1+o(1)))$: the
coefficient tail together with $(\mathrm{QG}_\lambda)$ forces zero displacement
at the single exponent, by Lemma~\ref{lem:tail} followed by
Lemma~\ref{lem:rouche}.
\end{corollary}

The open content consequently has two distinct layers: the quantitative
Galerkin estimates $(\mathrm{QG}_\lambda)$ that transport a known coefficient
tail to the continuum zero, and the following conjecture identifying the tail,
its archimedean rate, and the observed doubling beyond
Corollary~\ref{cor:single}.

\begin{conjecture}[Coefficient decay and zero displacement]\label{conj:decay}
Fix $\lambda^2$, let $q=q(\lambda^2)$ be the exponent of
Section~\ref{ssec:prime}, and let $\xi^{(\infty)}$ denote the limit
eigenvector at cutoff $\lambda^2$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{(Sharp coefficient tail, full form.)} There exist $c(\lambda^2)>0$ and
  $C$ with
  \[
    |\xi^{(\infty)}_k|\;\le\;C\,\exp\!\bigl(-c(\lambda^2)\,k^{q}\bigr).
  \]
\item[(ii)] \emph{(Leading rate.)} $c(\lambda^2)\,L/(2\pi)\to\delta=\pi/4$
  as $\lambda^2\to\infty$, with $\delta$ the $\Gamma_\mathbb{R}$ envelope
  rate of Section~\ref{ssec:arch}. The approach is from below: at finite
  cutoff the small-$k$ rate sits under $\delta$ and the deficit shrinks as
  the cutoff grows.
\item[(iii)] \emph{(Doubling at the zero.)} The first-zero displacement obeys
  \[
    |\nu_1(N,\lambda^2)-\nu_1(\infty,\lambda^2)|
    \;\le\;C\,\exp\!\bigl(-2\,c(\lambda^2)\,N^{q}(1+o(1))\bigr),
  \]
  twice the tail exponent of (i).
\end{enumerate}
\end{conjecture}

Three remarks delimit the clauses. Clause (i) is the coefficient-decay
formulation of Section~\ref{ssec:prime}; it is an ordinary Gevrey statement
only when $q\le1$. Clauses (ii) and (iii) together
recover the parameter-free slope~\eqref{eq:slope} at $q\approx1$, the
prediction validated to $3\%$ at $\lambda^2=7$; the finite-$\lambda^2$
coefficient rate ($\approx0.71$ at $\lambda^2=100$, against
$\delta\approx0.785$) is then a data point for the finite-cutoff deficit
that clause (ii) asserts, not a discrepancy. The author's coefficient-level
measurements across $\lambda^2=13$--$200$ show the small-$k$ rate climbing
toward $\pi/4$ as the cutoff grows, with the mid-band rate below it. What
controls the full profile is open and under study. Clause (iii) carries the doubling, and its
open content includes the factor $2$: conditional on
$(\mathrm{QG}_\lambda)$, Lemma~\ref{lem:tail} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:single} show that clause (i) implies the displacement bound
at the single exponent $c(\lambda^2)N^{q}$, while the observed
displacement decays at twice it. Conditional on global RH, Proposition~\ref{prop:dd} identifies the
finite eigenvalue-to-root transfer as first order in $\varepsilon_N$ with two
powers of a divided-difference norming factor. The strict fixed-cutoff claim in
clause~(iii) still requires the subtracted $N\to\infty$ identity together with
control of $\varepsilon_N-\varepsilon^\infty$ and the limiting norming terms;
the conditional identity supplies the root-transfer layer but does not remove
the Galerkin layer. None of the
clauses is a citation target: no classical result supplies the
$\Gamma_\mathbb{R}$ envelope for the finite ground-state transform (the
realization step, in which the finite transform inherits the completed
factor), so this is new analysis, precisely posed. \emph{Status: T3; it is
conditional on $(\mathrm{QG}_\lambda)$ and
Conjecture~\ref{conj:decay}. The general
simple-zero displacement Lemma~\ref{lem:rouche} remains T1. The open items are
typed new analysis in Table~\ref{tab:tiers}, with clause (ii) the specific home
of $\delta=\pi/4$ and clause (i) the sharp coefficient class shared with the exponent
row.}

\subsection{The prime correction: $q(\lambda^2)$}\label{ssec:prime}

The archimedean candidate gives the baseline $q=1$. The arithmetic part of the
localized Weil form is exactly a finite prime-power translation/cosine operator,
with frequencies $k\log p$ and weights involving
$(\log p)p^{-k/2}$ for $p^k\le\lambda^2$. It is \emph{not} known that the CCM
ground-state transform factorizes as a Gamma factor times a truncated Euler
product. To formulate a testable fluctuation model without assuming such a
factorization, isolate the first prime harmonics
$S_1(z)=\sum_{p\le\lambda^2}p^{-1/2}\cos(z\ln p)$. Their long-interval mean
square is
\[
  \langle S_1^2\rangle=\tfrac12\sum_{p\le\lambda^2}p^{-1}
  =\tfrac12(\ln\ln\lambda^2+M)+o(1)
\]
by Mertens' theorem ($M$ the prime Mertens constant). A separate classical
identity, Mertens' product theorem, gives
$\sum_{p\le\lambda^2}-\ln(1-1/p)=\gamma+\ln\ln\lambda^2+o(1)$.
The data show the fitted exponent decreasing as prime content grows. Motivated
by these two scales, but not derived from them, model the reciprocal exponent
as a baseline plus the prime fluctuation,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q}
  \frac1q = c_0+c_1\ln\ln\lambda^2,\qquad
  c_0=\gamma\ \text{(candidate baseline)},\quad
  c_1=\tfrac12\ \text{(candidate variance coefficient)},
\end{equation}
which gives $q(\lambda^2)=2/(2\gamma+\ln\ln\lambda^2)$. In this model the
numerator $2$ is $1/c_1$, while Mertens supplies the $\ln\ln$ scale. Neither
Mertens theorem supplies the missing operator map from prime fluctuations to a
sharp coefficient-decay index. The fixed operational expression is used on
the calibrated domain $7\le\lambda^2\le100$. Its denominator need not even
remain positive as $\lambda^2\downarrow1$, and the large-cutoff test of
Section~\ref{ssec:validation} shows that it also cannot be extrapolated
unchanged as $\lambda^2\to\infty$.

We test~\eqref{eq:q} against per-$\lambda^2$ fits of the full
form~\eqref{eq:nmodes} (Table~\ref{tab:q}). Restricting the quantitative
regression to the six cutoffs whose complete ramp configurations are preserved
by the reproduction suite, a linear regression
$1/q=c_0+c_1\ln\ln\lambda^2$ over $\lambda^2\in\{7,13,20,25,50,100\}$ yields
\[
  c_0=0.545\ (\gamma=0.577),\qquad c_1=0.489\ (\tfrac12=0.500),
  \qquad R^2=0.977 .
\]
Euler--Mascheroni $\gamma$ appears as the intercept of a regression that does
not assume it. The
offset of the parameter-free curve is systematic, not centered scatter.
Taking $c_0=\gamma$ exactly,
the idealized $q=2/(2\gamma+\ln\ln\lambda^2)$ runs $5$--$7\%$ \emph{below} the
per-$\lambda^2$ fits across the whole range (e.g.\ $1.10$ vs $1.185$ at
$\lambda^2=7$; $0.75$ vs $0.785$ at $\lambda^2=100$), and the regression
intercept $0.545$ sits about $5.6\%$ under $\gamma=0.577$, always on the same side. We
therefore report both curves side by side --- the provenance-complete fitted

$(\gamma,\tfrac12)$ --- and the identification of the intercept with $\gamma$
is a structural hypothesis the data supports to that one-signed accuracy, not
a measurement centered on it. \emph{Status: T3; the remaining lemma is a rigorous
variance-to-exponent map, now precisely posed on a Fourier coefficient-decay

\subsection{The Shannon coordinate}\label{ssec:coord}

\begin{lemma}[Conditional linear crossover scale]\label{lem:coord}
Assume the candidate leading prime ceiling and the candidate small-$N$ linear
slope of Sections~\ref{sec:primes} and~\ref{ssec:arch}. Their linear
intersection occurs at
\[
  N_{\mathrm{lin}}=\frac{D_{\mathrm{Primes}}}{\partial D/\partial N}
  =\frac{(4\pi/\ln10)\lambda^2}{\pi^2/(\ln10\,\ln\lambda^2)}
  =\frac{4}{\pi}\,\lambda^2\ln\lambda^2 .
\]
\end{lemma}

The $\lambda^2$ comes from the prime ceiling (Section~\ref{sec:primes}); the
$\ln\lambda^2$ from the inverse of the archimedean slope~\eqref{eq:slope}.
The combination $\lambda^2\ln\lambda^2$ is therefore the natural coordinate
within this two-input model. It is Shannon-like: the factor
$L=\ln\lambda^2$ converts a physical-frequency scale to a number of lattice
modes through the spacing $2\pi/L$. A literal identification with the Shannon
number of a specific prolate operator requires the still-open CCM/prolate
comparison; the classical time--band limiting results
\cite{LandauPollak1962,SlepianPollak1961,LandauWidom1980} motivate that
interpretation but do not supply it automatically.

The crossover multiplier and the fitted $\tanh$ coefficient must not be
conflated. Equation~\eqref{eq:nmodes} places $c$ \emph{inside} the argument, so
its unit-argument scale is
$N_{\tanh}=\lambda^2\ln\lambda^2/c$. Thus the observation
$c\approx4/\pi$ does not follow from
$N_{\mathrm{lin}}=(4/\pi)\lambda^2\ln\lambda^2$; one occurrence is a fitted
argument coefficient and the other is a crossover multiplier. Their relation
also depends on the stretched exponent and the chosen saturation convention.
Measured saturation occurs at
$N/(\lambda^2\ln\lambda^2)\approx2.0$--$2.3$, while the fitted onset
coefficient clusters near $c\approx1.27$ (Table~\ref{tab:q}). A second,
independent motivation comes from the geometric capacity of
Section~\ref{sec:background}. Ignoring the $7/8$ and oscillatory terms and
setting $K_{\max}^{(0)}(N,\lambda^2)\approx N$ gives
\[
 \log\!\left(\frac{N}{\ln\lambda^2}\right)-1\approx\ln\lambda^2,
 \qquad\text{hence}\qquad
 N\approx e\,\lambda^2\ln\lambda^2.
\]
This is a constraint-counting heuristic: it says that the transition should
occur when the number of geometrically available ordinate constraints is of
the same order as the basis dimension. It derives the coordinate and an
$O(1)$ multiplier from the paper's own capacity formula, but it does not prove
independence of the constraints or identify the empirical $\tanh$ convention.
Deriving $c$, or even proving its exact value is $4/\pi$, remains open.
\emph{Status: the algebra of the crossover lemma is T1 conditional on its T3
rate inputs; the capacity crossover is heuristic; the coordinate and the
fitted onset constant remain T3.}

\section{The prime ceiling $D_{\mathrm{Primes}}$}\label{sec:primes}

The prime channel of the intrinsic law~\eqref{eq:main-intrinsic} is the accuracy
at infinite $N$ and $P$, set solely by which primes $p\le\lambda^2$ enter the
form. It is the relative error of the first zero at the cutoff,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:epsprimes}
  \varepsilon_{\mathrm{primes}}(\lambda^2)
  =\frac{|\nu_1(\infty,\lambda^2)-\gamma_1|}{|\gamma_1|},
\end{equation}
with $\nu_1(\infty,\lambda^2)$ the first zero at infinite basis size and fixed
$\lambda^2$: it is what remains once modes and precision are exhausted, and its
digit measurement $D_{\mathrm{Primes}}=-\log_{10}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{primes}}$ is
the ceiling the minimum~\eqref{eq:main} consumes. We fix its leading rate through
the plunge \emph{eigenvalue}, the tractable route, kept here in digit space to
reinforce the intrinsic statement with the measured quantity.

The plunge eigenvalue gives an \emph{eigenvalue floor}
$D_{\mathrm{eig}}=-\log_{10}\varepsilon^\infty(\lambda)$, the digit value of the
smallest eigenvalue. Its prolate comparison requires two indexing conventions
to be kept separate. Let $\mu_n(c)$ denote the finite-Fourier singular value in
the full PSWF indexing and let $\Lambda_n(c)=\mu_n(c)^2$ be the corresponding
concentration eigenvalue. The function $h_{4,\lambda}$ has full index $n=4$;
in the even-sector notation of Connes~\cite[\S6.3]{Connes2026}, its positive
finite-Fourier eigenvalue is $\chi_2=\mu_4$. Thus it is not the principal
$n=0$ quantity $\chi_0$.

Fuchs' fixed-index theorem~\cite{Fuchs1964}, at
$c=2\pi\lambda^2$, gives
\[
  1-\Lambda_4(c)\sim \frac{2^{15}\sqrt{2}\,\pi^5}{3}\,
  (\lambda^2)^{9/2}e^{-4\pi\lambda^2}.
\]
Since $\mu_4=\sqrt{\Lambda_4}\to1$,
$1-\mu_4=(1-\Lambda_4)/(1+\mu_4)$, and therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prolatefloor}
  1-\chi_2(2\pi\lambda^2)\sim
  \frac{2^{14}\sqrt{2}\,\pi^5}{3}\,
  \exp\!\bigl(-4\pi\lambda^2+\tfrac92\ln\lambda^2\bigr).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the classical prolate digit scale is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eigfloor}
  \begin{aligned}
  D_{\mathrm{prol}}(\lambda^2)
    &:=-\log_{10}(1-\chi_2(2\pi\lambda^2))\\
    &=K\lambda^2-\frac{9}{2\ln10}\ln\lambda^2-C_0+o(1),
      \qquad K=\frac{4\pi}{\ln10}=5.45750\ldots.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where
$C_0=\log_{10}\!\bigl(2^{14}\sqrt{2}\,\pi^5/3\bigr)=6.3736\ldots$.
This fourth-index singular-value asymptotic is classical and T1. Groskin
\cite{Groskin2026} independently evaluates the same \S6.4 prefactor and obtains
$\log_{10}\approx6.37$. Hedenmalm's independent theta-kernel asymptotic also
contains the same $9/2$ logarithmic order~\cite{Hedenmalm2026}; it does not
supply the CCM-to-prolate transfer. The separate assertion that the CCM eigenvalue floor
$D_{\mathrm{eig}}$ follows this scale is the prolate-transfer step discussed
next; it is strongly supported by the empirical data but is not part of
Fuchs' theorem.

The variational part of the proposed transfer can be anchored rigorously, and
we record exactly that part as a proposition. Recall that the bottom of the spectrum of the continuum form is a
variational infimum, $\varepsilon^\infty(\lambda)=\inf_\varphi
QW_\lambda(\varphi,\varphi)/\|\varphi\|^2$, so \emph{any} trial vector bounds it
from above, irrespective of the sign of $\varepsilon^\infty$.

\begin{proposition}[Variational trial bound]\label{prop:variational}
Let $h_\lambda$ be the suitably normalized, zero-integral linear combination
of $h_{0,\lambda}$ and $h_{4,\lambda}$ used in the CCM prolate construction,
and let $k_\lambda=E(h_\lambda)$ be its image under the CCM Poisson-summation
map (\cite[\S7]{CCM2025}; \cite[\S6.4]{Connes2026}). Then, unconditionally,
\[
  \varepsilon^\infty(\lambda)\;\le\;
  R[k_\lambda]:=\frac{QW_\lambda(k_\lambda,k_\lambda)}{\|k_\lambda\|^2}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The bottom of the spectrum is the infimum of the Rayleigh quotient over the
form domain; any admissible trial vector bounds the infimum from above.
\end{proof}

\noindent The proposition is the variational principle verbatim and is T1.
It does not by itself identify the size of $R[k_\lambda]$. On the localized
form's exact split $QW_\lambda=A_{\mathrm{arch}}-A_{\mathrm{prime}}$
(archimedean part $w_{02}-w_r$, prime part the prime-power sum $w_p$;
\cite[\S6]{CCM2025}), define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rsplit}
  R[k_\lambda]
   =R_{\mathrm{arch}}[k_\lambda]-\rho_p[k_\lambda],
  \qquad
  \rho_p[k_\lambda]
   :=\frac{\langle A_{\mathrm{prime}}k_\lambda,k_\lambda\rangle}
           {\|k_\lambda\|^2}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Conditional CCM-to-prolate floor bound]\label{lem:floorbound}
Suppose, as $\lambda\to\infty$, that the constrained CCM trial vector satisfies
\[
  R_{\mathrm{arch}}[k_\lambda]
  \le (1-\chi_2(2\pi\lambda^2))(1+o(1))
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  \rho_p[k_\lambda]\ge0.
\]
Then
\[
  \varepsilon^\infty(\lambda)
  \le (1-\chi_2(2\pi\lambda^2))(1+o(1)).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By Prop.~\ref{prop:variational} and~\eqref{eq:Rsplit},
$\varepsilon^\infty(\lambda)\le R[k_\lambda]
\le R_{\mathrm{arch}}[k_\lambda]$ by the prime-sign hypothesis, and the stated
comparison with $1-\chi_2$ finishes the proof.
\end{proof}

\noindent The first hypothesis is the currently open CCM-to-prolate quotient
comparison. Connes~\cite[\S6.4]{Connes2026} reports the numerical similarity
between $\varepsilon^\infty(\lambda)$ and $1-\chi_2(\lambda)$ and describes
$k_\lambda$ as an educated approximation to the ground state; neither statement
is an exact Rayleigh-quotient identity. The second hypothesis is a sign on the
prime quotient evaluated on this same constrained trial vector. The paper's
measured prime contributions on the computed ground state support the sign but
do not prove it for $k_\lambda$.
Andrade's exact Loewner formula proves a termwise non-positive prime
contribution for the specific even vector $\cosh(x/2)$ and exposes mixed signs
in the odd sector~\cite{AndradePrime2026}. It does not prove the sign for the
constrained $h_0$--$h_4$ vector $k_\lambda$, but it sharpens the open task:
control the sign or weighted correlation of the exact Fourier symbol of this
particular trial state, rather than estimate the prime matrix entrywise.

Under both hypotheses, Eq.~\eqref{eq:prolatefloor} gives the conditional
one-sided floor bound
\[
  D_{\mathrm{eig}}(\lambda^2)\;\ge\;K\lambda^2-\frac{9}{2\ln10}\ln\lambda^2
  -C_0+o(1),\qquad K=\frac{4\pi}{\ln10},
\]
whenever $\varepsilon^\infty(\lambda)>0$. The empirical CCM calculations used
throughout this paper are unchanged and align with this fourth-index law; what
remains open is its analytic transfer from the constrained $h_0$--$h_4$ trial
construction to the full CCM floor. The reverse inequality,
that the ceiling cannot be beaten, i.e.\ a \emph{lower} bound on
$\varepsilon^\infty$ and on $|\nu_1(\infty,\lambda^2)-\gamma_1|$ of the same
exponential order, remains open, and is the archimedean--prime cancellation
of Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

Where finite-$\lambda^2$ control (rather than asymptotics) is needed, for
instance the curvature that produces the empirically steeper matching-floor
slope $P_m\approx5$ below, the modern non-asymptotic prolate literature
supplies two-sided eigenvalue bounds with explicit constants: the
three-region estimates of Bonami--Jaming--Karoui~\cite{BJK2021}, the improved
bounds of Karnik--Romberg--Davenport~\cite{KRD2021}, the upper bounds of
Osipov~\cite{Osipov2013}, the super-exponential decay estimates of
Bonami--Karoui~\cite{BonamiKaroui2017}, and Kulikov's exponential lower bound
before the plunge region~\cite{Kulikov2023}. These replace the
asymptotic-only inputs wherever a quantified inequality at a specific cutoff
is wanted.

The eigenvalue depth is a scaffold, not the object. What the law consumes is the
zero error $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{primes}}$, which sits a few digits below the
eigenvalue floor: converting the plunge depth $\varepsilon^\infty$ into the zero
displacement $|\nu_1-\gamma_1|$, and normalizing by $|\gamma_1|$ (the
$\log_{10}|\gamma_1|\approx1.15$ digits of the relative error), gives the matching
floor
\begin{equation}\label{eq:primes}
  D_{\mathrm{Primes}}(\lambda^2)=K\lambda^2-P_m\log_{10}\lambda^2-c_m,
  \qquad P_m\approx5,\quad c_m\approx9.5.
\end{equation}
The leading $K\lambda^2$ is shared with the eigenvalue floor, the same plunge
rate, so the intrinsic content is a \emph{single} channel, not two competing
ceilings; the subleading $P_m,c_m$ are the eigenvalue-to-zero conversion together
with the $|\gamma_1|$ normalization. The expanded artifact archive permits a
more direct test of that conversion. For each numerical configuration define
\[
  \mathrm{conv}_N(\lambda^2):=
  -\log_{10}|\varepsilon_N|-D_N,
\]
pairing the stored smallest Weil eigenvalue with the stored relative error of
the first refined root. On the deepest precision-clear configuration at each
of thirteen cutoffs, this gap grows from $2.499$ digits at $\lambda^2=7$ to
$2.925$ at $100$, then is $3.024$--$3.029$ throughout
$\lambda^2=500$--$1200$. Thus the earlier algebraic extrapolation
$(P_m-\tfrac92)\log_{10}\lambda^2+(c_m-C_0)$ is not supported as a growing
conversion law. The paired data instead favor a conversion approaching a
constant $C_{\mathrm{conv}}\approx3.03$ digits. If this persists in the
saturated continuum limit, then the asymptotic logarithmic coefficient returns
to $P_m=9/2$, while
$c_m=C_0+C_{\mathrm{conv}}\approx6.3736+3.03=9.40$, close to the finite-range
fit $c_m\approx9.5$. This is a sharper hypothesis, not yet a theorem: several
large-cutoff configurations are mode-limited, and a uniform target-transform
comparison is still required. The leading rate $K$ is derived for the
classical fourth-index deficiency and is observed for the CCM floor; the
transfer between those two quantities is the conditional step in
Lemma~\ref{lem:floorbound}. The
finite-range fit, hence $P_m$ and $c_m$, remains empirical; of it, the
$\approx1.15$-digit part of $c_m$ is the explicit $|\gamma_1|$ normalization and
the remainder is the depth-to-zero factor. Conditional on global RH, the finite conversion factor is no longer merely a
fitted constant: equation~\eqref{eq:convexact} below expresses
$D_{\mathrm{eig},N}-D_k$ exactly through the finite Stieltjes factor, divided
difference, and remote-zero term. That conditional finite identity does not
prove the saturated continuum limit, the large-cutoff constancy of
$C_{\mathrm{conv}}$, or the unconditional comparison needed in an RH argument.
A structural unconditional route to the limiting factor would require an
additional target-transform comparison, which we record as a proposal rather
than claim as a derivation. Specifically, let $Z_\lambda$ be a
compatibly normalized holomorphic target transform on a disc about $\gamma_1$,
with a simple zero at $\gamma_1$, and suppose one can prove
\[
  \eta_\lambda:=
  \sup_{\overline D(\gamma_1,r)}
  |\widehat\xi_\infty(z)-Z_\lambda(z)|
  \quad\text{is sufficiently small}.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:rouche}, now applied with $a=\gamma_1$,
$F=Z_\lambda$, and $G=\widehat\xi_\infty$, would then give
\[
  |\nu_\infty(\lambda)-\gamma_1|
  \le \frac{2\eta_\lambda}{|Z_\lambda'(\gamma_1)|}.
\]
The determinant identity
$\det{}_{\!\mathrm{reg}}(D-z)=-i\lambda^{-iz}\widehat\xi(z)$ of
\cite[Thm.~5.10]{CCM2025} describes the finite transform, but does not itself
construct $Z_\lambda$ or prove this uniform estimate. Establishing that
comparison, connecting $\eta_\lambda$ to the plunge depth, and deriving the
$\lambda$-scaling of the ratio would promote $(P_m,c_m)$ from empirical to
structural. Until then the row remains T4.

The data indicate that the arithmetic does not move the eigenvalue-floor slope.
A formal large-$|t|$ model motivated by Mertens' theorems and
prime-number-theorem partial summation has the scalar form
$S(t)=\tfrac12 t^2-\gamma|t|+c_0$. Turning this scalar asymptotic into a
statement about the compressed CCM prime operator requires its own operator
limit: parity, the mean-zero constraint, and the compression $Q$ do not by
themselves justify assigning the three displayed terms separate actions on the
finite-section ground state. The screw-function decomposition of
Suzuki~\cite{Suzuki2026} suggests a useful comparison with the metric operator,
while the exact finite prime-power translations provide a second, independent
route through quadratic-form or Loewner estimates. Either route could explain
why the arithmetic changes lower-order conversion terms without changing the
prolate prefactor power, but that conclusion is not yet a theorem. Conditional on the transfer in
Lemma~\ref{lem:floorbound}, the prefactor power is the Fuchs index $\tfrac92$ of
the fourth-index prolate model (Section~\ref{sec:primes}), while the arithmetic
contribution enters the lower-order conversion. The archimedean part of the
form does not by itself carry the
plunge (it is $O(1)$-indefinite; see the rate-origin discussion in
Section~\ref{sec:discussion}). The empirically steeper
matching-floor slope $P_m\approx5$ is a finite-$\lambda^2$ curvature effect, not a
change in the asymptotic slope.

\emph{Status: the classical fourth-index prolate coefficient $K$ and slope
$\tfrac92$ are T1. Their transfer to the CCM eigenvalue floor is T3: it is
validated by the data, while the constrained-trial comparison and the prime
sign in Lem.~\ref{lem:floorbound} remain open. The reverse inequality requires
new archimedean--prime cancellation estimates. The matching-floor offset
($P_m$, $c_m$), which also contains the eigenvalue-to-zero conversion, remains
empirical (T4). The paired artifacts now isolate a candidate constant
$C_{\mathrm{conv}}\approx3.03$ and favor the asymptotic value $P_m=9/2$; the
structural route remains the target-transform comparison named above.}

\paragraph{A smooth envelope with arithmetic microstructure, not a staircase.}
A natural hypothesis, that $D_{\mathrm{Primes}}$ changes by macroscopic steps
when a prime power enters, is falsified directly. With $N$ and $P$ held above
floor ($N=300$, $P=200$), sweeping $\lambda^2$ in unit steps from $4$ to $30$
gives an overall rise of $\approx5.3$ digits per unit $\lambda^2$; at that
resolution the per-unit rate at prime-power entries is statistically
indistinguishable from non-prime entries. This does not mean the exact matrix
path is smooth across an entry event. Groskin proves that its first derivative
has a rank-one jump at every prime power~\cite{GroskinMatrix2026}; small local
bumps or kinks are therefore expected. The empirical conclusion is the more
precise one: Eq.~\eqref{eq:primes} describes a smooth macroscopic envelope, not
a distinct digit staircase, while the arithmetic events survive as finer path
structure.

\paragraph{Floor anchors.}
Floor-isolated measurements (both $N$ and $P$ above floor) give
$D_{\mathrm{Primes}}(100)=526.08$ at $(N,P)=(1000,700)$ and a saturated
$D_{\mathrm{Primes}}(200)\approx1070.4$ at $N\ge2400$. The latter sits at the
matching floor $K\cdot200-5\log_{10}200-9.5=1070.5$, confirming the two-floor
structure at the largest cutoff measured. Regarding provenance, the $\lambda^2=200$
anchor was measured against the $2500$-digit Arb reference of
Definition~\ref{def:D}, not a shorter tabulation. A matching-digit count cannot exceed
the length of its reference: against a $\sim1000$-digit reference the
measurement would \emph{saturate} near $999$ and could never read
$1070.4$, so the observed value is itself incompatible with a
reference-limited artifact. Such an artifact would present as a suspicious
plateau at the reference length, not a reading $70$ digits past it.

\section{The Riemann--von Mangoldt backbone and the spectral-edge
hypothesis}\label{sec:equilibrium}

Sections~\ref{sec:nmodes} and~\ref{sec:primes} approach the mode ramp and the
prime ceiling by two separate classical routes: the Landau--Widom transition
and the Fuchs prolate tail. Their shared scales and the data suggest that they
are two regimes of one CCM spectral edge. This section separates the rigorous
smooth counting backbone from that stronger, offensive hypothesis.

\subsection{The rigorous archimedean counting backbone}\label{ssec:eqmeasure}

Let
\[
 \vartheta(T)=
 \operatorname{Im}\log\Gamma\!\left(\frac14+\frac{iT}{2}\right)
 -\frac{T}{2}\log\pi
\]
be the Riemann--Siegel phase in the standard continuous branch. The
Riemann--von Mangoldt formula can be written
\[
 N_\zeta(T)=\frac{\vartheta(T)}{\pi}+1+S(T),
\]
away from ordinates, with the usual limiting convention at a zero. Stirling's
formula gives the unconditional smooth density
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rvm}
 \rho_{\mathrm{RvM}}^{(0)}(T)
 :=\frac{\vartheta'(T)}{\pi}
 =\frac{1}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}+O(T^{-2}).
\end{equation}
Thus the archimedean Gamma phase supplies the smooth Riemann--von Mangoldt
backbone, while $S(T)$ contains the oscillatory correction. This statement is
independent of RH and does not require an equilibrium-measure interpretation.
Evaluating the integrated smooth density at the exact finite-band edge
$T_{\max}=2\pi N/\ln\lambda^2$ recovers the leading terms of
$K_{\max}^{(0)}$ in~\eqref{eq:kmax}. The reach formula and its smooth capacity
therefore remain rigorous independently of the conjectural edge picture.

The earlier formal identification of~\eqref{eq:rvm} with the minimizer of an
unscaled unit-mass logarithmic energy is not valid: the density has infinite
mass on the half-line, and differentiating a weighted-energy Euler--Lagrange
condition produces a singular Hilbert-transform equation rather than
$\rho=Q'/\pi$. A genuine equilibrium formulation must specify a scaling
window, a normalized empirical measure, its external field, and which spectral
objects are being counted. Weighted logarithmic-energy theory
\cite{SaffTotik1997} and its orthogonal-polynomial/random-matrix realization
\cite{Deift1999} provide candidate tools once those CCM-specific objects are
derived; their general results do not supply the missing identification by
themselves.

\subsection{A scaled equilibrium conjecture}\label{ssec:eqconjecture}

For a fixed $0<a<1$, a candidate CCM zero measure in a high-frequency window is
\[
 \mu_{T,a}^{\mathrm{CCM}}
 :=\frac{1}{K_{T,a}}
   \sum_{aT<\nu_k\le T}\delta_{\nu_k/T},
\]
where $K_{T,a}$ is the number of selected transform zeros in the window. Its
smooth Riemann--von Mangoldt target is the probability measure
\[
 d\mu_{T,a}^{(0)}(x)
 :=\frac{T\rho_{\mathrm{RvM}}^{(0)}(Tx)\,\mathbf1_{[a,1]}(x)\,dx}
 {\int_{aT}^{T}\rho_{\mathrm{RvM}}^{(0)}(t)\,dt}.
\]
These definitions do not assert that the CCM zeros form a logarithmic gas;
they state a normalized object on which such a theorem could be formulated.

\begin{conjecture}[Scaled CCM equilibrium and common edge]
Along a joint regime in which the cutoff and finite-section reach tend to
infinity:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] the selected CCM zero measures have the same macroscopic limit as
  $\mu_{T,a}^{(0)}$;
\item[(ii)] there are scaled external fields for which these measures are
  asymptotically characterized by a weighted logarithmic-energy problem; and
\item[(iii)] the observed mode transition and prime tail arise as two scales of
  one associated CCM spectral edge.
\end{enumerate}
\end{conjecture}

The most direct offensive route uses the determinant identity
$\det_{\mathrm{reg}}(D''-z)=-i\lambda^{-iz}\widehat\xi(z)$. Numerical and
regularized Fredholm determinant theory supplies templates for controlling
finite determinants and their product anomalies~\cite{Bornemann2008,GalloZweckLatushkin2025,BritzEtAl2020}. A limit for the
normalized subharmonic functions
\[
 \frac{1}{K}\log\left|\det_{\mathrm{reg}}(D''-z)\right|
\]
would determine a limiting zero measure through its distributional Laplacian
and could identify the external field and logarithmic potential. This route
addresses transform zeros directly and supplies the missing bridge between the
finite determinant and a potential-theoretic measure.

A complementary route begins with the archimedean matrix. Its explicit phase
rate $\vartheta'(t)=\tfrac12\log(t/2\pi)+O(t^{-2})$ suggests a
Toeplitz-, Kac--Murdock--Szeg\H{o}-, or pseudodifferential-type spectral law
\cite{KMS1953,Widom1982}. To make that route rigorous one must first identify
the exact matrix class and normalization, handle the unbounded logarithmic
symbol, verify form-core and spectral-distribution hypotheses, and obtain a
uniform estimate for the arithmetic perturbation. Even then, a separate theorem
must connect the resulting matrix eigenvalue measure to the transform-zero
measure. The discreteness result of~\cite[Thm.~3.6]{CCM2025} motivates this
program but does not by itself establish all of these quantitative steps.

\subsection{One-edge conjecture: two observed scales}\label{ssec:oneedge}

In the parameter range where the relevant Weil form is positive, the computed
spectrum descends from an $O(1)$ bulk into a deep plunge. Two classical facts
make a common-edge interpretation compelling:
\begin{itemize}
\item the Landau--Widom transition width~\cite{LandauWidom1980} supplies the
  natural crossover scale near the Shannon number
  $\lambda^2\ln\lambda^2$, matching the observed mode ramp;
\item the fourth-index Fuchs asymptotic~\cite{Fuchs1964} supplies the extreme
  prolate tail, matching the observed leading scale of the prime ceiling.
\end{itemize}
The classical plunge is commonly written as a Fermi--Dirac/logistic
profile; after centering and rescaling, a logistic is algebraically a shifted
$\tanh$. This makes the empirical choice of a hyperbolic tangent less ad hoc,
but Landau--Widom's index variable is not yet the CCM root-error coordinate.
Modern pre-plunge estimates sharpen that warning: before the transition, the
natural variable contains a logarithmically corrected distance to the Shannon
edge rather than a pure normalized power~\cite{KulikovSharp2026,Azimifard2026}.
The measured failure of the unchanged $q_\gamma$ model at
$\lambda^2=1000$ therefore elevates a log-corrected plunge coordinate from an
optional analogy to a leading empirical competitor. Identifying the CCM
variable, including whether the correction should be assigned to $q$, to $c$,
or to the coordinate itself, remains part of the open edge comparison rather
than a consequence of the classical profile.
The offensive hypothesis is that these are the transition and extreme tail of
the same CCM edge, explaining the algebra
$D_{\mathrm{nModes}}=D_{\mathrm{Primes}}\,g$. This is not yet a consequence of
the Riemann--von Mangoldt bulk density: the Weil-matrix spectrum, the perturbed
operator spectrum, the transform zeros, and the prolate concentration spectrum
must first be connected by explicit comparison theorems. The microscopic
universality class (Airy, Bessel, or Landau--Widom), the determinant-to-measure
bridge, and the exact deep-edge constant remain open. \emph{Status: T3 --- a
structural conjecture supported by two classical asymptotic regimes and the
measured convergence surface; proving it requires new potential-theoretic and
spectral-comparison analysis.}

\subsection{Convergence criterion}\label{ssec:criterion}

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-cutoff branch convergence]\label{prop:limit}
Fix $\lambda>1$. Assume the spectral and zero-branch hypothesis
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poshyp}
  (\mathrm{H}_\lambda^\ast):\quad
  \begin{cases}
  QW_\lambda \text{ has a simple, isolated, positive lowest eigenvalue }
       \varepsilon^\infty(\lambda),\\
  \text{its normalized continuum eigenvector }\xi_\infty
       \text{ is even},\\
  \widehat\xi_\infty \text{ has a simple isolated positive zero }
       \nu_\infty(\lambda)\ne\gamma_1 .
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Let $\xi_N$ be the phase-aligned finite-section ground states and let $\nu_N$
denote the zero of $\widehat\xi_N$ selected in a sufficiently small disc about
$\nu_\infty$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] The lowest Rayleigh values converge monotonically to
  $\varepsilon^\infty(\lambda)$, while
  $\xi_N\to\xi_\infty$ in norm and
  $\widehat\xi_N\to\widehat\xi_\infty$ compact-uniformly.
\item[(ii)] For all sufficiently large $N$, the selected zero $\nu_N$ is unique
  in that disc and $\nu_N\to\nu_\infty(\lambda)$.
\item[(iii)] If the numerical root perturbation vanishes as $P\to\infty$, then
  \[
    \lim_{\substack{N\to\infty\\P\to\infty}}
    D(\lambda^2,N,P)
    =D_{\mathrm{Primes}}(\lambda^2)
    :=-\log_{10}\frac{|\nu_\infty(\lambda)-\gamma_1|}{|\gamma_1|}
    <\infty .
  \]
  Consequently, at this fixed cutoff the selected branch cannot converge to
  $\gamma_1$ merely by increasing $N$ and $P$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The Rayleigh-value statement is \cite[Prop.~3.4]{CCM2025}. Under the
simple/isolated/even hypotheses in~\eqref{eq:poshyp},
\cite[Thm.~6.1]{CvS2025} gives convergence of the finite-section eigenvectors
and compact-uniform convergence of their transforms. Choose a closed disc about
$\nu_\infty$ containing no other zero of $\widehat\xi_\infty$. Since that zero
is simple, Hurwitz's theorem (equivalently the local Rouch\'e argument of
Lemma~\ref{lem:rouche}) gives one zero $\nu_N$ in the disc for all sufficiently
large $N$ and $\nu_N\to\nu_\infty$. Vanishing numerical perturbation then gives
the same limit as $P\to\infty$, and continuity of $-\log_{10}|\cdot|$ away from
zero proves part~(iii).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Growing cutoffs.}
For a sequence $\lambda_j\to\infty$, convergence to $\gamma_1$ follows if the
three separately defined errors vanish:
\[
 |\nu_{N_j}(\lambda_j)-\nu_\infty(\lambda_j)|\to0,\qquad
 \varepsilon_{\mathrm{round}}(P_j)\to0,\qquad
 |\nu_\infty(\lambda_j)-\gamma_1|\to0.
\]
This is a sufficient pathwise criterion from the triangle inequality. The
budget choices
$N_j\gtrsim(4/\pi)\lambda_j^2\ln\lambda_j^2$ and
$P_j\gtrsim K\lambda_j^2$, and the rate
$D\sim K\lambda_j^2$, are the quantitative model tested in this paper; they do
not follow from the qualitative fixed-cutoff theorem above. Conversely, ruling
out convergence for an arbitrary family of cutoffs in a bounded interval would
require a uniform separation
$\inf_\lambda|\nu_\infty(\lambda)-\gamma_1|>0$ on that interval, which is not
claimed here.

\paragraph{Status of the hypothesis $(\mathrm{H}_\lambda^\ast)$.}
We state $(\mathrm{H}_\lambda^\ast)$ explicitly because finite-cutoff Weil
positivity is \emph{not} known unconditionally for all $\lambda$:
\cite{CCM2025} is explicit that positivity of the continuum measure cannot be
asserted in general, and positivity for all $\lambda$ is precisely the open
conjecture linking the construction to RH. What is unconditional is the
small-support range: Yoshida proved the Weil hermitian form positive definite
for test functions of sufficiently small support~\cite{Yoshida1992}, Bombieri
reproved and quantified this in his study of the truncated
functional~\cite{Bombieri2000}, Connes--Consani gave the operator-theoretic
strengthening at the archimedean place via the Sonin
space~\cite{ConnesConsaniSelecta}, and the $\zeta$-cycles
paper~\cite{ConnesConsaniZC} tracks finite-$\lambda$ positivity prime by prime
through the first cutoffs. Suzuki~\cite[Thm.~1.4]{Suzuki2026} also proves a
positive, simple, even continuum ground state for sufficiently small aperture.
Andrade extends the Perron argument to the \emph{pole-free} continuum operator
at every aperture and reduces the remaining pole term, which is rank one in
each parity sector, to a scalar resolvent/nodal question~\cite{AndradePole2026}.
The same obstruction can be sharpened to a scalar inequality between two
Herglotz/Weyl functions~\cite{AndradeHerglotz2026}. These are precise attacks
on full even-simplicity, not proofs of it. These
results address only part of~$(\mathrm{H}_\lambda^\ast)$: simplicity of
the selected continuum-transform zero remains an explicit hypothesis.
Beyond the rigorous small-aperture regime, positivity, simplicity, and evenness
are supported numerically at every cutoff measured in this program
($\lambda^2\le200$, and $\lambda^2\le1000$ in~\cite{AndrewsA}). The finite
matrices $(\lambda^2,N)=(13,120),(29,160),(53,200)$ additionally have
independent Arb certificates that the lowest even-sector eigenvalue is simple
and strictly below the lowest odd-sector eigenvalue~\cite{AndradeEven2026}.
Those certificates strengthen three finite data points but do not establish
uniformity or the continuum limit. The
noncoincidence $\nu_\infty(\lambda)\ne\gamma_1$ is likewise numerical and is
what makes the fixed-cutoff limiting digit count finite.

\noindent The qualitative division of labor is now explicit. CCM
Prop.~3.4 supplies monotone convergence only for the lowest Rayleigh values.
Connes--van Suijlekom Thm.~6.1 supplies conditional eigenvector and
compact-uniform transform convergence, and simplicity of the selected
continuum zero supplies the zero branch. No monotonicity of root accuracy and
no pointwise bound $D\le D_{\mathrm{Primes}}$ for every finite $N$ is asserted:
finite-$N$ and cutoff displacements can partially cancel. The quantitative
growing-cutoff rate inherits the separate rigor of $D_{\mathrm{Primes}}$: the
classical fourth-index prolate asymptotic is T1, while its transfer to the
constrained CCM trial quotient is T3 and the continuum plunge remains an
assumption.

Conceptually, along the selected branch the fixed-cutoff limit isolates the
cutoff displacement once precision and Galerkin errors are driven to zero.
Reaching $\gamma_1$ therefore requires a growing-cutoff path on which the
continuum-transform zero itself approaches $\gamma_1$. The measured model
$D\sim K\lambda^2$ quantifies the proposed cost along such a path --- each
additional digit demands a fixed increment $\ln10/(4\pi)$ in $\lambda^2$ ---
but this quantitative rate is not supplied by the qualitative fixed-cutoff
theorem.


\subsection{Conditional Gram representation and divided-difference root transfer}
\label{ssec:gramdd}

The operational law above is empirical. This subsection gives a separate,
conditional explanation of the finite eigenvalue-to-root conversion that the
law currently records through a conversion gap. It is placed after the formula
and its validation because the formula does not depend on the hypothesis used
here.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{$(\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{RH}})$} \emph{The Riemann Hypothesis. Ordinates
$\gamma$ are counted with multiplicity. Simplicity is required only where a
simple-zero derivative or a one-to-one zero label is used.}
\end{quote}

The hypothesis is global: the sums below run over all nontrivial zeros, so a
finite-height verification does not replace $(\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{RH}})$ at
the depths considered here. We do not assign a numerical off-line remainder
in this paper.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional Gram representation]
\label{prop:gram}
Let $V_N=\operatorname{span}\{V_j:|j|\le N\}$ and let $f\in V_N$ have
$\|f\|=1$. The finite Guinand--Weil dictionary and support exhaustion at
cutoff $\lambda^2$ give the explicit-formula zero sum for
$F=f\star\widetilde f$~\cite{GroskinGW2026}. Under
$(\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{RH}})$ it becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gram}
 QW_\lambda(f,f)=\sum_{\gamma\in\pm\Gamma}|\widehat f(\gamma)|^2,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_N(\lambda)=
 \min_{\substack{f\in V_N\\ \|f\|=1}}
 \sum_{\gamma\in\pm\Gamma}|\widehat f(\gamma)|^2 .
\end{equation}
The series is absolutely convergent for the finite-mode test functions used
here.
\end{proposition}

Equation~\eqref{eq:gram} is a Christoffel-type extremal formulation of the
finite eigenvalue depth. It unifies the eigenvalue-depth analogues of the two
intrinsic channels: changing $N$ changes the trial space, while changing
$\lambda^2$ changes the exponential type and the exhausted prime content.
Root digits still require a separate eigenvalue-to-root transfer.

\begin{proposition}[Divided-difference root transfer]
\label{prop:dd}
Assume $(\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{RH}})$. Let $\xi$ be the unit ground vector of the
finite form, with
\[
 \widehat\xi_N(z)=2L^{-1/2}\sin(zL/2)G_N(z),\qquad
 G_N(z)=\sum_{|j|\le N}\frac{\xi_j}{z-\alpha_j},\qquad
 \alpha_j=\frac{2\pi j}{L}.
\]
Let $\nu$ be a real zero of $G_N$ and define
$\eta_j=\xi_j/(\alpha_j-\nu)$. Then $\eta\in V_N$ and
$\widehat\eta(z)=\widehat\xi_N(z)/(z-\nu)$. Testing the finite eigenvalue
equation against $\eta$ gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dd-sum}
 \sum_{\gamma\in\pm\Gamma}
 \frac{|\widehat\xi_N(\gamma)|^2}{\gamma-\nu}
 =\varepsilon_N S_N(\nu),
 \qquad
 S_N(\nu)=\sum_{|j|\le N}\frac{\xi_j^2}{\alpha_j-\nu}.
\end{equation}
For the zero $\nu_k$ matched to the positive ordinate $\gamma_k$, set
\[
 B_{k,N}=\frac{\widehat\xi_N(\gamma_k)}{\gamma_k-\nu_k},\qquad
 R_{k,N}(\nu)=\sum_{m\ne k}
 \frac{2\nu\,|\widehat\xi_N(\gamma_m)|^2}{\gamma_m^2-\nu^2}.
\]
Pairing the ordinates $\pm\gamma_m$ in~\eqref{eq:dd-sum} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dd-exact}
 \boxed{
 \gamma_k-\nu_k=
 \frac{\gamma_k+\nu_k}{2\nu_k}\,
 \frac{\varepsilon_N S_N(\nu_k)-R_{k,N}(\nu_k)}{|B_{k,N}|^2}.}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The partial-fraction identity
\[
 \frac{1}{(z-\alpha_j)(z-\nu)}
 =\frac{1}{\alpha_j-\nu}
 \left(\frac{1}{z-\alpha_j}-\frac{1}{z-\nu}\right)
\]
shows that $G_N(z)/(z-\nu)$ is again a lattice sum; the apparent extra pole
vanishes because $G_N(\nu)=0$. Thus $\eta\in V_N$ and the matrix identity
$\langle QW_\lambda\xi,\eta\rangle=
\varepsilon_N\langle\xi,\eta\rangle$ becomes~\eqref{eq:dd-sum} by
Proposition~\ref{prop:gram}. Isolating the $\pm\gamma_k$ pair gives
\eqref{eq:dd-exact}; no remainder is discarded.
\end{proof}

Define the finite eigenvalue depth
$D_{\mathrm{eig},N}=-\log_{10}|\varepsilon_N|$ and
\[
 \widetilde S_{k,N}=S_N(\nu_k)-\frac{R_{k,N}(\nu_k)}{\varepsilon_N}.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:dd-exact} gives the exact relative conversion gap
\begin{equation}\label{eq:convexact}
 \boxed{
 D_{\mathrm{eig},N}-D_k=
 \log_{10}\!\left(
 |\widetilde S_{k,N}|\,
 \frac{\gamma_k+\nu_k}
 {2\gamma_k\nu_k|B_{k,N}|^2}\right).}
\end{equation}
When $\nu_k\approx\gamma_k$ and
$B_{k,N}\approx\widehat\xi_N'(\nu_k)$, this becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:convapprox}
 D_{\mathrm{eig},N}-D_k\approx
 \log_{10}\!\left(
 \frac{|\widetilde S_{k,N}|}
 {\gamma_k|\widehat\xi_N'(\nu_k)|^2}\right).
\end{equation}
The remote term requires a truncated zero sum with a controlled tail; any
published numerical use of it must state the truncation height, zero source,
and tail estimate.

\paragraph{Relation to the doubling conjecture.}
The exact identity is linear in $\varepsilon_N$ and contains two powers of a
norming divided difference. It therefore identifies the conditional
first-order mechanism for the total displacement $\nu_k(N)-\gamma_k$.
Conjecture~\ref{conj:decay}(iii), however, concerns
$\nu_k(N)-\nu_k(\infty)$ at fixed cutoff. Promoting the measured doubled
finite-basis exponent still requires subtracting the limiting identity and
controlling
\[
 \varepsilon_N-\varepsilon^\infty,\qquad
 S_N-S_\infty,\qquad B_{k,N}-B_{k,\infty},\qquad
 R_{k,N}-R_{k,\infty}.
\]
Thus Proposition~\ref{prop:dd} supplies the root-transfer layer of the
$(\mathrm{QG}_\lambda)$ program; it does not remove the Galerkin layer.

\paragraph{Independent numerical check.}
On the independent implementation, the two sides of the first-order balance
at $(\lambda^2,N,k)=(7,40,1)$ agree in ratio $0.999996$. The exact conversion
formula reproduces predicted/measured gaps $2.4689/2.4689$ at $(7,40)$,
$2.69434/2.69435$ at $(13,80)$, and $2.78431/2.78432$ at $(20,144)$.
Across $k\le12$ at $\lambda^2=13,20$,
$\gamma_kS_N(\nu_k)\in[-1.07,-1.00]$, while the remote share decreases from
$1.4\%$ to $0.15\%$ along the three increasingly saturated configurations.
These are T3 measurements from a high-precision numerical implementation, not
interval certificates, and should be replayed from the primary Rust artifacts
before final publication.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Formula first.}
The empirical and analytic questions are deliberately separated. The first
question is whether a caller who supplies $(\lambda^2,N,P_{\mathrm{req}})$
receives an accurate digit prediction. Tables~\ref{tab:fixed-validation} and
\ref{tab:large-cutoff} answer that question directly: the fixed published
submodel is accurate to about one digit on independent ramps through
$\lambda^2=20$, remains useful with a residual of about six digits at the edge of the
calibrated domain, and fails decisively when extrapolated unchanged to
$\lambda^2=1000$. The second question is why the working formula has its
observed form. The Gram, Galerkin, prolate, determinant, and prime-event
programs address that second question; none is allowed to redefine the
caller-facing law without a better whole-cutoff validation result.

\paragraph{Relation to Groskin~\cite{Groskin2026}.}
Groskin independently implemented the Connes--van Suijlekom matrix and measured
a Galerkin convergence exponent $s(c)$ growing with the cutoff, proposing a
Paley--Wiener analyticity-strip mechanism. The present work builds on that
mechanism: Prop.~\ref{prop:stretched}(ii) gives the precise local-slope
coordinate in which his $s(c)$ can be compared with our measured
stretched-exponential. Establishing that the eigenvalue and root experiments
sample the same asymptotic tail remains part of the transfer problem. The
candidate strip rate is the archimedean $\Gamma_\mathbb{R}$ value
$\delta=\pi/4$. What is new here is the digit-space
formulation, the Shannon coordinate, the calibrated closed form
$q_\gamma(\lambda^2)$, and the embedding of the rate in the full
three-budget minimum. A second independent reimplementation reproduced the
fixed-form ramps at $\lambda^2=7,13,20$ and supplied the large-cutoff
constraint in Section~\ref{ssec:validation}; this corroborates the law on its
stated domain while preventing an unqualified extrapolation.

Groskin's revised analysis, extended to cutoff $\lambda^2=100$, is independent
corroboration of this two-regime structure. He finds that no single
smooth-convergence law fits his eigenvalue-space data and explicitly declines to
assert a convergence rate, reporting instead an unresolved tension between a
\emph{drifting} effective exponent and an apparent \emph{finite} convergence
limit. Both halves have a common empirical interpretation here:
Prop.~\ref{prop:stretched}(ii) explains how a stretched exponential produces
the drift (its local exponent
$2s_{\mathrm{loc}}(N)=aqN^{q}$ grows with $N$), while the prime ceiling of
Section~\ref{sec:primes} supplies the finite limit $D_{\mathrm{Primes}}(\lambda^2)$.
What looks like an irreconcilable choice within a single-rate framework is simply
the pre-saturation ramp and the ceiling of the minimum law~\eqref{eq:main}, seen
in one dataset.

\paragraph{Relation to \'Sliwi\'nski~\cite{Sliwinski2026} and Suzuki~\cite{Suzuki2026}.}
\'Sliwi\'nski proves an inverse-logarithmic lower bound on the \emph{mean}
spectral error over the whole spectrum (Thm.~3.1), a different regime from the
first-zero convergence here, and consistent with it, since the mean is dominated
by the unconverged high-index eigenvalues while $\nu_1\to\gamma_1$ converges
super-exponentially. Suzuki's screw-function analysis supplies the archimedean
$\ln|z|$ multiplier and the prime-frequency support that underlie
Sections~\ref{ssec:arch}--\ref{ssec:prime}.

\paragraph{Origin of the leading rate: arithmetic, not archimedean.}
The prime ceiling is \emph{modeled} by an archimedean object, the prolate
eigenvalue of Section~\ref{sec:primes}, yet the exponential rate is not carried
by the archimedean part of the Weil form itself. Diagonalizing the
screw-function split~\cite{Suzuki2026} at high precision, the archimedean-only
localized form is $O(1)$-indefinite, with no exponentially small eigenvalue.
Restricting it to the Sonin subspace, the orthogonal complement of the
band-concentrated prolate modes, where Connes--Consani establish archimedean Weil
positivity~\cite{ConnesConsaniSelecta} (surveyed in~\cite{Connes2026}),
restores positivity, but only as an $O(1)$ gap:
the smallest Sonin eigenvalue is $2.26$, $2.28$, $2.57$ at $\lambda^2=5,8,13$,
essentially flat in $\lambda^2$, against a floor $K\lambda^2=27,44,71$. A
direct diagonalization of the archimedean-only form at $\lambda^2=13$--$50$
finds the same structure from the eigenvector side: the lowest even mode is a
delocalized Sonin-type state with an $O(1)$ eigenvalue, so the
band-concentrated plunge eigenvector exists only for the full form.
Archimedean positivity and the plunge are therefore \emph{decoupled}: the place
at infinity supplies an $O(1)$ spectral gap, while the exponentially small
$\varepsilon^\infty\sim e^{-4\pi\lambda^2}$ is produced by the cancellation
between the archimedean and prime parts of the full form. This locates one of the
analytic steps behind the leading coefficient: a quantitative
archimedean--prime cancellation estimate on the even ground eigenvector. The
preceding constrained CCM-to-prolate quotient comparison is a separate open
step, which is why the full CCM identification of $K$ is~T3.

\paragraph{Boundary spectral mass as a proof route.}
For a unit finite ground state let
$w_0=|\langle(2N+1)^{-1/2}\mathbf1,u_0\rangle|^2$. Across $56$ independent
configurations at $\lambda^2\in\{7,13,20\}$, the ratio
$\varepsilon_N/w_0$ remains between $0.398$ and $1.762$ while
$\varepsilon_N$ changes by more than $25$ decimal orders. Because
$\sum_j\xi_j$ is proportional to the boundary value of the finite Fourier
state, this gives a Bonami--Karoui-type boundary-action interpretation of the
depth~\cite{BonamiKaroui2017}. The identity and its normalization should be
derived explicitly before it carries theorem-level weight; at present it is
one promising analytic attack on the mode law, not a replacement for it.

\paragraph{Next formula-selection experiment.}
The large-cutoff data do not yet choose among a corrected exponent, a changing
onset coefficient, or a new coordinate. The next validation pass will compare
five fixed candidate surfaces by leaving out entire cutoffs: the current
$q_\gamma$ model; the fitted linear relation for $1/q$; a saturated exponent;
a logarithmically corrected pre-plunge coordinate; and a two-stage prediction
$D_{\mathrm{eig},N}-C_{\mathrm{conv}}$. The selected replacement, if any,
must improve the $\lambda^2=150,200,250,500,1000$ holdouts without degrading
the existing $\lambda^2\le100$ surface.

\paragraph{Practical use.}
On the calibrated domain, the law is a parameter-selection guide: to target
$D^\ast$ digits, pick $\lambda^2\gtrsim D^\ast\ln10/(4\pi)=D^\ast/5.46$ so the
ceiling clears $D^\ast$; use equation~\eqref{eq:nmodes} to choose $N$ so the
ramp clears $D^\ast$; and choose $P_{\mathrm{req}}$ so the precision budget is
nonbinding. Increasing any one parameter past the binding minimum yields
nothing. At cutoffs beyond $100$, the minimum and cutoff budgets remain useful,
but the closed $q_\gamma$ mode map must not be used for planning until the
large-cutoff transition model is replaced or directly calibrated.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We have given a single law~\eqref{eq:main} for the convergence of the CCM
zeta spectral triple. In it the minimum structure is forced; the measured prime
ceiling has leading coefficient $4\pi/\ln10$, matching the rigorous
fourth-index prolate singular-value asymptotic. Its transfer through the
constrained CCM trial state and the reverse inequality are open; and the mode budget, the convergence rate
named open in~\cite{CCM2025}, is measured as the logarithm of a
stretched-exponential. The fixed operational map
$q_\gamma=2/(2\gamma+\ln\ln\lambda^2)$ with $c=4/\pi$ predicts independent
ramps through $\lambda^2=20$ to approximately one digit RMS and remains the
validated caller-facing formula on $7\le\lambda^2\le100$. A new
$\lambda^2=1000$ sweep rejects its unchanged extrapolation and places the
effective exponent in the convention-dependent range $0.74$--$0.80$; the data
do not yet decide whether the correction belongs to the exponent, onset
coefficient, or coordinate. Identifying the calibrated root-error exponent
with a sharp eigenvector coefficient class remains open, and direct fits to
finite archived eigenvectors show substantial $N$- and window-dependence that
any continuum theorem must explain.
The rigorous Riemann--von Mangoldt backbone
and the measured convergence surface motivate the conjecture that the mode ramp
and prime ceiling are two scales of one CCM spectral edge
(Section~\ref{sec:equilibrium}). Under $(\mathrm{H}_\lambda^\ast)$,
the selected finite-section zero converges to the fixed-cutoff continuum zero.
Along a growing-cutoff path, vanishing numerical, Galerkin, and cutoff
displacements is sufficient for convergence to $\gamma_1$; the explicit rate
$D\sim(4\pi/\ln10)\lambda^2$ remains the quantitative model tested here, not a
consequence of the fixed-cutoff theorem. Each claim is stated with an explicit rigor
level. The rigorous core (the minimum bound, the precision slope, the
stretched-exponential reparametrization and local-slope identities of
Prop.~\ref{prop:stretched}, and the general simple-zero displacement
Lemma~\ref{lem:rouche}) stands on its own. Conditional on global RH,
Proposition~\ref{prop:dd} adds an exact finite divided-difference formula for
the eigenvalue-to-root conversion; it explains the measured conversion gap but
is not part of an unconditional route to RH. The tail-to-transform mechanism is
retained in Lemma~\ref{lem:tail} conditional on the explicit quantitative
Galerkin estimates $(\mathrm{QG}_\lambda)$; proving those estimates is a named
route to promotion rather than a discarded line of attack. The variational bound of
Prop.~\ref{prop:variational} supplies the unconditional trial inequality but
does not identify its quotient with the prolate deficiency; and the
remaining open items are typed in Table~\ref{tab:tiers}. Of these, the
\emph{named lemmas} (including the constrained-trial comparison and prime-sign
hypothesis behind Lem.~\ref{lem:floorbound}) are precisely posed inside
classical theories and are natural next steps; the \emph{new-analysis} items
(the determinant/logarithmic-potential limit and spectral comparisons behind
the common-edge conjecture, the coefficient-decay estimate for
the $\Gamma_\mathbb{R}$-weighted form (Conjecture~\ref{conj:decay}), the
coefficient-class subordination behind $q$,
the archimedean--prime cancellation needed for the reverse cutoff asymptotic,
and positivity beyond the Yoshida range) are where the construction's genuinely
open mathematics lives.
The paired eigenstate/root archive also isolates a more economical candidate
for the finite-cutoff offset: the depth-to-relative-root conversion approaches
about $3.03$ digits over the largest precision-clear cutoffs sampled. If that
constant persists after saturation, it favors the asymptotic logarithmic
coefficient $P_m=9/2$ and explains most of $c_m\approx9.5$ without a growing
conversion penalty. This is a new empirical constraint, not a proof of the
target-transform comparison.
Separately, the exact finite-band edge~\eqref{eq:tmax} and its
Riemann--von Mangoldt capacity~\eqref{eq:kmax} quantify how many zeta ordinates
are geometrically available before questions of convergence quality arise.

\section{Explicit non-claims}\label{sec:nonclaims}

The per-component rigor of the law is the subject of Table~\ref{tab:tiers} and
is not repeated here. Beyond it, we are explicit about three boundaries.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{No proof of the Riemann Hypothesis.} We characterize how the
  CCM/CvS approximation converges; the link from the construction to RH,
  continuum positivity of $QW_\lambda$ for all $\lambda$, is the open
  conjecture of~\cite{CCM2025,CvS2025,Connes2026}, and is neither assumed nor
  advanced here.
\item \textbf{We do not establish that the construction reproduces the zeta
  zeros.} That the finite operator's zeros converge to the true ordinates as
  $\lambda^2\to\infty$ is the construction's own open premise; we quantify the
  approach to $\gamma_1$ \emph{under} that premise through the growing-cutoff
  criterion following Prop.~\ref{prop:limit}, and do not prove the premise
  itself.
\item \textbf{Scope.} The law is stated and tested for the first zero $\gamma_1$
  of the Riemann zeta function. We make no claim about its constants for higher
  zeros, nor about extension to Dirichlet $L$-functions or other settings. The
  reach quantities $T_{\max}$ and $K_{\max}^{(0)}$ count the frequency band and
  its expected zero occupancy; they are not asserted to be a higher-zero
  convergence or correct-ordering theorem.
  The fixed operational constants $(q_\gamma,c)$ are validated on
  $7\le\lambda^2\le100$. Historical reference values at
  $\lambda^2=150,200$ remain excluded until their exact ramp provenance is
  recovered. The closed map has now been tested at $\lambda^2=1000$ and fails
  there: all fitting conventions give an effective exponent near
  $0.74$--$0.80$, rather than $q_\gamma(1000)=0.648$, and the unchanged formula
  overpredicts the measured ramp by more than $100$ digits. We therefore make
  no claim that the calibrated $q_\gamma$ or $c=4/\pi$ persists at large
  cutoff. The minimum-budget structure, cutoff ceiling, and stretched-ramp
  family are separate claims and are not retracted by this failure.
\item \textbf{No unconditional general finite-cutoff hypothesis.} Statements
  requiring the full spectral and zero-branch hypothesis
  $(\mathrm{H}_\lambda^\ast)$ are conditional. Yoshida and Bombieri establish
  its positivity component in the small-support range
  \cite{Yoshida1992,Bombieri2000}, and Suzuki establishes a positive, simple,
  even continuum ground state for sufficiently small aperture
  \cite[Thm.~1.4]{Suzuki2026}; simplicity and noncoincidence of the selected
  transform zero remain explicit assumptions. We do not claim positivity of
  $QW_\lambda$ for general $\lambda$.
\end{itemize}

This appendix records the two structural facts about $\widehat\xi$ used in the
body, the closed form of the transform (Lemma~\ref{lem:sampling}) and
entirety/reality of its zeros, in a form that depends only on elementary
computation and on the self-contained proofs of~\cite{CCM2025}. Its purpose is
to make explicit that no statement in this paper routes through the
Carath\'eodory--Fej\'er machinery of~\cite{CvS2025}, which we cite as the
historical origin of the reality mechanism only.

\subsection{The closed form of the transform, from scratch}\label{app:closedform}

Write $t=\ln u\in[-L/2,L/2]$, so that
$V_n(u)=L^{-1/2}e^{2\pi i n(t+L/2)/L}=L^{-1/2}(-1)^n e^{2\pi i nt/L}$ and the
Fourier--Mellin transform is the Fourier integral over the interval. For a
single mode,
\begin{align*}
  \widehat V_n(z)&=\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}e^{izt}\,V_n(t)\,dt
  =L^{-1/2}(-1)^n\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}e^{i(z+2\pi n/L)t}\,dt\\
  &=L^{-1/2}(-1)^n\,\frac{2\sin\!\bigl((z+2\pi n/L)\,L/2\bigr)}{z+2\pi n/L}.
\end{align*}
Since $\sin(zL/2+\pi n)=(-1)^n\sin(zL/2)$, the sign cancels:
\[
  \widehat V_n(z)=\frac{2L^{-1/2}\sin(zL/2)}{z+2\pi n/L}.
\]
Summing over the eigenvector $\xi=\sum_{|n|\le N}\xi_n V_n$ and reindexing
$j=-n$ (using $\xi_{-j}=\xi_j$ for the even eigenvector, or simply relabeling)
gives
\[
  \widehat\xi_N(z)=2L^{-1/2}\sin(zL/2)\sum_{|j|\le N}\frac{\xi_j}{z-2\pi j/L},
\]
the closed form of Lemma~\ref{lem:sampling}, in agreement with
\cite[Prop.~5.9]{CCM2025}. The derivation is a one-line Fourier integral; no
external input is used. Entirety is immediate: each apparent pole
$z=2\pi j/L$ is cancelled by the zero of $\sin(zL/2)$ there (the removable-pole
computation in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:sampling}), so $\widehat\xi_N$ is
entire of exponential type $L/2$.

\subsection{Reality of the zeros: provenance}\label{app:reality}

The statement that, in the critical case, all zeros of $\widehat\xi_N$ are real
and coincide with the spectrum of the perturbed operator is
\cite[Thm.~5.10]{CCM2025}. We record why its proof is self-contained there.
The proof chain consists of: (a) \cite[Lem.~5.2]{CCM2025}, the observation that
the relevant matrices have the structure $\tau_{ij}=(b_i-b_j)/(i-j)$; (b)
\cite[Lem.~5.4]{CCM2025}, the elementary commutator identity
$DT-TD=|\beta\rangle\langle\eta|-|\eta\rangle\langle\beta|$ for such matrices,
with $D$ the diagonal mode operator; and (c) the conclusion of
\cite[Thm.~5.10]{CCM2025}: when $\varepsilon_N$ is the smallest eigenvalue with
even simple eigenvector, $T=QW^N_\lambda-\varepsilon_N\ge0$, the perturbed
operator is self-adjoint in the $T$-inner product, and the transform is
proportional to a regularized characteristic determinant,
$\det_{\mathrm{reg}}(D''-z)=-i\lambda^{-iz}\widehat\xi(z)$, whose zeros are
therefore real, being the characteristic polynomial of a self-adjoint matrix. Each
of (a)--(c) is proven within~\cite{CCM2025} by direct computation and does not
require the general Carath\'eodory--Fej\'er extension of~\cite{CvS2025}. The
distinct qualitative passage from finite sections to the continuum eigenstate
and its transform zeros in Prop.~\ref{prop:limit} does invoke
\cite[Thm.~6.1]{CvS2025} under $(\mathrm{H}_\lambda^\ast)$.

\subsection{The independent classical foundation}\label{app:foundation}

For completeness we record the pre-CvS lineage on which the truncated Weil
form itself rests: Weil's positivity criterion; Yoshida's unconditional
positivity for test functions of sufficiently small
support~\cite{Yoshida1992}; Bombieri's study of the quadratic functional
restricted to a finite interval, its minimizer in the $L^2$ unit ball, and the
eigenvalues of its finite truncations~\cite{Bombieri2000}; the
operator-theoretic strengthening of Yoshida's theorem at the archimedean place
by Connes--Consani~\cite{ConnesConsaniSelecta}; the prolate identification of
the small eigenvectors in the $\zeta$-cycles paper~\cite{ConnesConsaniZC}; and
Suzuki's screw-function framework~\cite{SuzukiJLMS,SuzukiHilbert,Suzuki2026},
which unifies Yoshida, Bombieri, and the Connes--Consani(--Moscovici) results
by means of continuous functions and de~Branges-space methods. The
construction studied in this paper can be founded entirely on this lineage
together with~\cite{CCM2025}.
